# US state privacy laws  (as of 2026-10-10)

> **Verify before relying.** State laws are amended every session and thresholds change. This table is a starting point for
> triage, not a compliance answer. Column "Checked" says how each row was confirmed on 2026-10-10:
> **official** = the statute, enrolled act or legislature/AG page was read (what was read is in parentheses); **secondary** =
> consistent law-firm/tracker reports only (official page blocked or JS-only; treat those numbers as (unverified) until you open the
> statute). Read the current statute before you rely on any threshold, date, cure period or exemption.

## Comprehensive consumer privacy laws
Thresholds are per calendar year and count residents of that state only (not employees/B2B contacts in most laws; California counts both).
"Sale %" = share of gross revenue from selling personal data. GPC = must honor a universal opt-out signal (Global Privacy Control).

## States without a comprehensive law (as of 2026-10)
- **Ohio**: no comprehensive consumer privacy law. Earlier Ohio Personal Privacy Act bills (HB 376 in the 134th GA, HB 345 in the
  135th) died in committee; the 136th GA has no consumer-rights bill (HB 801 "Ohio Privacy Act" concerns giving personal data to
  out-of-state entities; HB 798 "Privacy Protection Act" is unrelated) (legislature.ohio.gov listings via search, secondary).
  Applicable Ohio law: Consumer Sales Practices Act (R.C. 1345), breach notice (R.C. 1349.19: within 45 days of discovery; 1,000+
  residents -> notify consumer reporting agencies; AG enforces), and the **Data Protection Act safe harbor (R.C. 1354)**: an
  affirmative defense to Ohio tort claims after a breach if the business kept a written cybersecurity program reasonably conforming
  to a listed framework (NIST CSF/800-171/800-53, FedRAMP, CIS Controls, ISO 27000 family; PCI DSS only combined with another),
  sized to the business; creates no private right of action (1354.04) (codes.ohio.gov read 2026-10-10). No Ohio auto-renewal statute
  found (unverified); CSPA still reaches deceptive renewals.
- **North Carolina**: no comprehensive law. NC Consumer Privacy Act bills: S 757 (2025, in committee), S 1022 (filed 2026-04-30,
  re-referred to Senate Appropriations 2026-05-05, no further action) (ncleg.gov). Applicable: UDAP (N.C.G.S. 75-1.1), Identity
  Theft Protection Act breach notice (N.C.G.S. 75-65: without unreasonable delay; also notify the AG Consumer Protection Division;
  1,000+ -> CRAs too; content list in the statute), auto-renewal (N.C.G.S. 75-41: clear disclosure, cancellation instructions,
  written notice 15-45 days before renewal of terms over 60 days, changed terms in 12-point bold; a violation voids the renewal
  clause unless the seller shows routine compliance procedures and refunds) (ncleg.gov read 2026-10-10).
- Others without a comprehensive law (2026-10, secondary): e.g., Pennsylvania, Massachusetts, New York (SHIELD Act for security and breach; Child Data Protection Act, unverified), Michigan, Wisconsin, Georgia. Re-check each session.

## Universal opt-out signals (GPC)
- Required to honor (secondary unless noted): California (official), Colorado, Connecticut, Texas, Oregon, Montana, Delaware,
  New Hampshire, New Jersey, Minnesota, Maryland, Nebraska, plus newer laws. Detect `Sec-GPC: 1` header / `navigator.globalPrivacyControl`
  and apply opt-out of sale/sharing/targeted ads for that browser (and the account if logged in, per CA regs).
- California AB 566 (signed 2025-10, operative 2027-01-01): browsers must offer a signal (law-firm reports; leginfo blocked).
- If you have no sale, sharing or targeted ads, say so and still state how GPC is treated.

## Sensitive data and minors (high-level)
- Sensitive data (health, precise geolocation, race/ethnicity, religion, sexual orientation, citizenship/immigration status,
  genetic/biometric, children's data, and increasingly neural data and transgender/nonbinary status) needs **opt-in consent** in
  most laws; California uses a "limit" right instead. Maryland bans sale outright; Oregon (2026-01-01), Virginia (2026-07-01) and
  Connecticut (2026-10-01) ban the sale of precise geolocation data (Oregon official; the others secondary).
- Consumer health data: Washington My Health My Data Act (private right of action; geofencing ban) and Nevada SB 370 apply to
  small companies too (secondary). Separate health-data policy page required in Washington.
- Minors: several laws add teen consent for targeted ads/sale (CT, NJ 13-16, OR under-16 sale ban, MD under-18).
- **Age-appropriate design and social media laws are in litigation.** California AADC: *NetChoice v. Bonta* (9th Cir. 2026-03-12,
  date on CourtListener): the data-use limits and dark-pattern ban stay enjoined as likely vague; the injunction on the coverage
  definition and age estimation was lifted and those issues went back to the district court; the 2024 injunction on impact
  assessments stands (holding per law-firm reports). Nothing is enforced yet. Maryland Kids Code, Vermont and Nebraska design codes,
  and state social-media age laws face NetChoice suits; status changes month to month (unverified).
- **App Store Accountability Acts**: Texas SB 2420 in force (Fifth Circuit stay pending appeal 2026-06-04, No. 26-50001; Supreme
  Court denied vacatur 2026-07-06, No. 25A1390, docket read; Apple applies it to new Texas accounts from 2026-06-04, Apple news).
  Utah: HB 498 (2026) moved key duties to 2027-05-06 and left only a private right of action (secondary). Louisiana: HB 977 (Act 185,
  signed 2026-05-15) moved the start to 2027-07-01 (secondary). Alabama HB 161 signed 2026-02-17, start 2027-01-01 (secondary).
  California AB 1043 (OS age signal; developers must request it) operative 2027-01-01 (secondary). Developers must request the age
  signal and get parental consent for "significant changes".

## Breach-notice laws (all 50 states, DC, PR, USVI, Guam)
How to check, per incident: (1) list affected residents by state; (2) open each state's statute on the legislature site (not a
chart) for: definition of personal information (some include login credentials, biometrics, health), encryption safe harbor,
deadline (fixed days in many states, e.g., Ohio 45; "without unreasonable delay" in others such as NC), AG/regulator notice and
threshold, CRA notice threshold, notice content; (3) check sector rules (HIPAA, GLBA, FTC HBNR 60 days); (4) check contracts.
NCSL keeps a list of statutes (lead only). Don't promise a fixed notice time in a policy.

## California must-haves (website or app with California users)
- CalOPPA: posted privacy policy, effective date, how you respond to Do Not Track (secondary).
- If CCPA applies: notice at collection (categories, purposes, sale/share, retention per category), privacy policy with all
  required sections, methods to submit requests (two or more, including a toll-free number unless online-only with direct
  relationship), "Do Not Sell or Share" and "Limit" links or frictionless GPC, verification, 45-day response (+45), annual update,
  service-provider contracts, metrics if you handle 10M+ consumers.
- Shine the Light (Civ. Code 1798.83) if you share for third parties' direct marketing (secondary).
- Auto-renewal law (Bus. & Prof. 17600+, as amended by AB 2863 for contracts from 2025-07-01; secondary, leginfo blocked) for subscriptions; Delete Act/DROP only for registered data brokers.
