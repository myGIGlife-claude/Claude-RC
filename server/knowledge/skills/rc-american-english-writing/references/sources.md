# Sources and what they are good for

Accessed 2026-10-10 unless noted. "Read" means the page text was actually retrieved and used. Everything else is a pointer only.

## Read
- https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language (the federal plain-language guide; the old plainlanguage.gov address redirects here): audience-first writing, content design, testing for understanding, basis in the Plain Writing Act of 2010. The page itself is an overview; the detailed rules are in the linked guides.
- https://developers.google.com/style/highlights (page updated 2025-04-02): friendly conversational tone, active voice, address the reader as "you", sentence-case headings, serial commas, unambiguous dates, alt text.
- https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/welcome/ (page dated 2025-01-08): voice described as warm and relaxed, crisp and clear, ready to help; links to bias-free and global-communication guidance.

## Proprietary or not inspected (use the user's copy or ask; never fabricate rule numbers)
- The Chicago Manual of Style, the AP Stylebook, Garner's Modern English Usage, the American Heritage Dictionary and its usage notes, Purdue OWL. Which governs: AP for news and much marketing and PR; Chicago for books and long-form; a company or project guide beats both; a form, statute or contract follows its own rules.

## How to settle a usage question
1. The user's project or brand guide, if any. 2. The governing style guide, if the user has it. 3. A current dictionary entry (look it up; do not trust memory for borderline words). 4. If authorities split, say so and pick the plainer option. Mark any disputed or unchecked point as such.
