# Official sources by level, and how to get the version in force on a date (checked 2026-10-10)

Use the official source first. A free finder (CourtListener, Google Scholar, Cornell LII, Justia) is fine for locating text; confirm
anything you cite against the official version when one exists, and say which one you used.

## Federal
| Need | Official source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Statutes (current) | uscode.house.gov (Office of the Law Revision Counsel) | Up on 2026-10-10 (current through Pub. L. 119-118) after a brief maintenance notice; fall back to govinfo when it is down. Positive-law titles are legal evidence of the law; other titles are only prima facie evidence and the Statutes at Large govern (govinfo help page). |
| Statutes (historical) | govinfo.gov US Code collection (1994 to present, annual editions + supplements); uscode.house.gov "prior release points" | Before 1994: Statutes at Large and print editions (law library, HeinOnline (paid)). Read the public law that amended the section and its effective-date note. |
| Public laws, bills, legislative history | congress.gov; govinfo.gov (Statutes at Large, public laws) | A bill is not law. Check "became public law" and the enacted text. congress.gov blocked automated fetches on 2026-10-10 (open it in a browser). |
| Regulations (current) | ecfr.gov | Unofficial editorial compilation, current within about two business days (Office of the Federal Register). |
| Regulations (historical) | ecfr.gov point-in-time and compare-dates (back to January 2017; eCFR reader aid "Using the eCFR Point-in-Time System", read 2026-10-10); govinfo.gov annual CFR editions and the Federal Register | The official CFR and Federal Register on govinfo give legal notice; eCFR does not. |
| Court rules | uscourts.gov current rules; pending amendments page | The Supreme Court transmits rules to Congress by May 1 and they take effect no earlier than December 1 of that year unless Congress acts (28 U.S.C. 2074(a), text read 2026-10-10; 2072 is the enabling act itself and has no dates). Then the district's local rules and the judge's standing orders. |
| Opinions and dockets | supremecourt.gov; each circuit and district court site; PACER (paid per page); CourtListener RECAP (free, partial) | Supreme Court bound volumes (U.S. Reports PDFs) are on supremecourt.gov. |

## Ohio
| Need | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Revised Code | codes.ohio.gov | Each section shows effective date, latest legislation, an authenticated PDF, and "Available Versions of this Section" with links. How far back versions go varies by section (unverified; older text needs session laws or archived print). |
| Administrative Code | codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code | Check the rule's effective date and five-year review date. |
| Court rules | supremecourt.ohio.gov, Laws and Rules, Ohio Rules of Court | Civ.R., App.R., Juv.R., Evid.R., Sup.R. Local rules are on each court's own site. |
| Opinions | supremecourt.ohio.gov Rulings and Opinions | Ohio Official Reports for appellate opinions since 2012-07-01 is the Supreme Court website (Rep.Op.R. 3.2); bound volumes control for accuracy where they exist (Rep.Op.R. 4.2); cite with paragraph numbers. |
| Citing appellate opinions | Rep.Op.R. 3.4 | Opinions issued after 2002-05-01 may be cited regardless of publication; weight is for the court. A Supreme Court refusal to take a discretionary appeal says nothing on the merits (Rep.Op.R. 4.1). |

## North Carolina
| Need | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| General Statutes | ncleg.gov, Laws, General Statutes | Showed "changes through S.L. 2026-30" on 2026-10-10; links to law changes not yet reflected; Statutes Archive for prior versions; site carries a not-official caveat. |
| Session Laws | ncleg.gov, Session Laws | The enacted text and its effective-date section decide timing. |
| Court rules, opinions, forms | nccourts.gov | HTML pages blocked automated fetches on 2026-10-10 (the rule PDFs under /assets/inline-files/ did download); open in a browser. Appellate rules: the official codified PDF from the Supreme Court's rules page. Unpublished Court of Appeals opinions: N.C. R. App. P. 30(e)(3) (not controlling; citation disfavored except preclusion or law of the case; allowed with a served copy when no published opinion serves as well; text read in the 2023-03-01 codification, re-check the current one). |

## Other states
Legislature site (statutes, session laws, archives), the state supreme court site (rules, opinions), the official administrative code,
the secretary of state (entities), and each trial court's local rules. Uniform Law Commission texts are models, not the enacted law:
always read the state's enacted version.

## Free finders (locate, then confirm)
- CourtListener: large free opinion collection, RECAP federal dockets, "cited by" lists, citation lookup API (free token).
- Google Scholar case law: full text and "How cited"; no negative-treatment flags.
- Cornell LII: US Code, CFR, federal rules with committee notes, Supreme Court opinions.
- Justia: opinions and state codes (code copies may lag; check the official site).
None of these is a citator. Their silence does not mean a case is still good law.

## Getting the law in force on a past date
1. Find the current section and its history line (effective date, amending acts).
2. Open the prior version covering the date (Ohio "Available Versions", NC Statutes Archive, govinfo annual US Code edition, eCFR
   point-in-time or the annual CFR).
3. If no online version covers the date, read the amending session law and work backward; say the reconstruction is yours.
4. Check transition or savings clauses: an amendment may apply only to orders, cases or conduct after a date.
5. Record: version used, its effective window, URL, verified-on date.
