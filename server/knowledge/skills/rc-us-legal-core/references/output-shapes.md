# Output shapes

Do the full research and audit internally; deliver the smallest shape that answers the task. Always keep VERIFIED FACTS, APPLICABLE LAW,
ARGUMENTS, RISKS and NEXT STEPS separate (headings in a memo; short labeled paragraphs in a chat answer).

## Short answer (chat)
```
Short answer: <yes / no / it depends on X>. (One line: I'm not a lawyer; this is research, not legal advice.)
What the law says: <rule in one or two sentences> (<cite>, checked YYYY-MM-DD).
What decides it for you: <1-3 facts, marked verified/alleged>.
Risks: <main counterpoint or deadline>.
Next step: <one concrete action>; <when to talk to a <state> <area> lawyer and what to bring>.
```

## Research memo
Question presented / Short answer / Facts (verified; alleged; assumed) / Applicable law (as of the relevant date; as of today) /
Analysis (element by element) / Counterarguments and adverse authority / Risks and uncertainty / Next steps (with deadline labels) /
Authorities (records with verified-on dates; unverified items listed separately).

## Filing draft package
1. Cover note to the user (not filed): what this is, open issues, deadline labels, the audit failures still open.
2. The draft in court format: caption with placeholders, numbered paragraphs if the court uses them, headings per argument, record and
   exhibit cites, specific requested relief, signature block left blank for the signer, blank certificate of service with instructions,
   proposed order if the court expects one.
3. "Items you must verify or complete before filing" list (SKILL.md section 8).

## Plain-English explainer for a non-lawyer
Lead with what happens next and what they must do by when; define each legal term once in plain words; no citations in the body
(put them at the end); keep the limit statement to one sentence.
