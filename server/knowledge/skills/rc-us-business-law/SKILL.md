# US business law: entities, contracts, workers  (as of 2026-10)

> Research aid, not legal or tax advice, and no attorney-client relationship. Every fee, form and deadline below is dated; re-check the
> official source on the day you rely on it. Anything marked (unverified) is a lead, not a fact. State law decides most of this: name the
> state before answering. Tax questions go to a CPA or tax attorney; securities, patents and anything litigated go to a licensed lawyer.

Detail lives in `references/`: `entity-comparison.md` (entity table, when each fits), `nc-oh-de-formation.md` (fees, statutes, annual
duties, foreign qualification, other states), `contract-clauses.md` (contract law points and a clause checklist per contract type),
`worker-classification.md` (federal and NC/OH tests, consequences). Policies, privacy terms and litigation are out of scope here.

## Currency check
- **Beneficial ownership (CTA/BOI)**: FinCEN's **final rule, announced 2026-08-11, published and effective 2026-08-14 (91 FR 52508)**,
  makes the March 2025 interim rule permanent. US-formed companies are **not** reporting companies and file nothing; no company reports
  US-person owners or applicants; US persons with a FinCEN ID need not update it. Only entities formed under foreign law and registered
  to do business in a US state still report (non-US owners and applicants only). Interim rule: announced 2025-03-21, published
  2025-03-26. The final rule's preamble says FinCEN **intends** to delete BOI of US companies and US persons in one sweep, with no
  confirmations, and does not anticipate deleting such data included in filings made after 2027-02-10 (final rule text; fincen.gov/boi
  says nothing on deletion, 2026-10-10). It is a stated plan, not a rule text: don't promise a client their data is gone.
- **State transparency laws fill the gap**: New York's LLC Transparency Act took effect **2026-01-01**. Its "reporting company" is the
  federal CTA definition limited to LLCs formed or authorized in NY (LLC Law §1106(b)); NY DOS says only LLCs **formed under a foreign
  country's law** and authorized in NY report (NY-formed and other-US-state LLCs are not reporting companies); the governor vetoed the
  bill that would have covered NY LLCs (2025-12-19, law-firm reports). LLCs authorized before 2026-01-01 file by **2026-12-31**, new
  ones within 30 days of the application for authority (§1107(d)-(e)); exempt companies file an exemption attestation; $25 per filing;
  AG fines up to $500/day when past due (dos.ny.gov FAQ, 2026-10-10).
- **Non-competes**: the FTC rule is **not in effect and not enforceable** (FTC page, 2026-10-10): set aside by a federal district court
  2024-08-20; FTC moved to drop its appeal 2025-09-05; Federal Register removal notice 2026-02-12 (91 FR 6507). The FTC now attacks
  overbroad non-competes case by case as unfair methods of competition under FTC Act §5 (Gateway Services, a pet-cremation company:
  complaint and proposed order 2025-09-04, 90 FR 43606; consent order finalized **2025-11-26**, FTC press release). State law governs
  enforceability.
- **FLSA contractor test**: DOL's 2024 six-factor rule (eff. 2024-03-11) is still on the books; **FAB 2025-1 (2025-05-01)** told WHD to
  stop applying it in investigations. **NPRM announced 2026-02-26, published 2026-02-27 (91 FR 9932)** would return to the 2021 "core
  factors" (control, profit/loss) and extend it to FMLA and MSPA; comments closed 2026-04-28; **no final rule in the Federal Register as
  of 2026-10-10**. Courts apply their own circuit's economic-reality test regardless.
- **1099 threshold**: Form 1099-NEC/MISC reporting threshold rose from $600 to **$2,000** for tax years beginning after 2025 (OBBBA
  §70433; IRS 1099-MISC/NEC instructions, 2026-10-10), inflation-indexed from 2027.
- **UCC 2022 amendments** (hybrid goods/services rule, "record" replaces "writing", new Article 12 controllable electronic records):
  **Ohio HB 195 effective 2026-10-06** (R.C. 1302.04 now says "record"); **North Carolina** via S.L. 2025-25 (G.S. 25-2-102 hybrid rule).
- **Delaware SB 21** (signed 2025-03-25, 85 Del. Laws c. 6): DGCL §144 safe harbors for interested-director and controlling-stockholder
  deals (disinterested committee or stockholder approval, or fairness; both for going-private) and a defined "controlling stockholder";
  §220 lists the "books and records" a stockholder may demand, with a particularity and confidentiality regime (delcode text, 2026-10-10).
- **Delaware fees**: Division of Corporations fee schedule **revised 2026-08-01**: LLC certificate of formation $110, corporation from $109,
  foreign LLC registration $200, foreign corp qualification $245, name reservation $75; LLC/LP/GP annual tax $400 due June 1.
- **North Carolina**: SOS warns of "priority annual report" vendor mailings. S.L. 2026-59 §§12-16 (eff. **2026-10-01**): an entity more
  than 50% owned by a deployed service member that files an affidavit of deployment **before** deployment gets relief: corporations and
  LLPs may file the annual report within 90 days after deployment ends, LLCs by April 15 of the following year; fee waived (ncleg text;
  the SOS page summarizes it as "90 days", 2026-10-10).
- **Ohio CAT**: gross-receipts exclusion $6M for 2025 and later ($3M for 2024); annual minimum tax gone for periods from 2024-01-01;
  at or under the exclusion no return is due (Ohio Dept. of Taxation information release CAT 2023-01, a 2023 scanned PDF; the
  tax.ohio.gov CAT pages returned 404 to this session 2026-10-10: confirm current thresholds there).
- **USPTO**: trademark base application fee $350 per class (electronic) for applications filed on or after 2025-01-18 (89 FR 91062,
  2024-11-18); the same rule adds per-class surcharges for incomplete applications and free-form goods/services text (amounts unverified).
- **FTC "click-to-cancel"** amendments to the Negative Option Rule were vacated by the 8th Circuit on 2025-07-08 (*Custom
  Communications v. FTC*, No. 24-3137, law-firm reports); the FTC recodified the pre-2024 rule text 2026-02-12 (91 FR 6507).
  ROSCA, the 1973 rule and state auto-renewal laws still apply to SaaS subscriptions.

### Older versions (law in effect at the time)
- A contract signed, a worker paid or an entity formed in the past is judged under the law **then** in force. Ohio LLCs formed before
  2022-02-11 were under R.C. Chapter 1705; Chapter 1706 now governs all Ohio LLCs (1705 repealed effective 2022-02-11, see R.C. 1706.83).
- BOI reports filed in 2024-2025 under the original 2023 rule: no update duty now for US companies; don't tell a client they were
  "never required" (they were, until 2025-03-26).
- Pre-2026 1099s use $600. Pre-2026-10-06 Ohio sales contracts: old UCC Article 2 text. Old non-competes: test them under the state law
  and statute versions in force when signed, plus any later statute that says it applies retroactively.

## What changed / stop doing
| Stop | Do instead | Since |
|---|---|---|
| "Every new LLC must file a BOI report within 30/90 days" | US-formed entities file nothing with FinCEN; check state laws (e.g. NY for foreign-formed LLCs) | IFR 2025-03-26; final rule eff. 2026-08-14 |
| "The FTC banned non-competes" | No federal ban; state law + FTC §5 case-by-case scrutiny of overbroad clauses | rule set aside 2024-08-20; appeal dropped 2025-09 |
| Citing the 2024 DOL contractor rule as the enforcement standard | Cite the circuit's economic-reality test; note the 2026 NPRM and check for a final rule | FAB 2025-1 (2025-05-01); NPRM 2026-02-27 |
| 1099-NEC at $600 for 2026 payments | $2,000 (indexed from 2027) | OBBBA, tax years beginning after 2025 |
| "LLC vs S corp" as alternative entities | LLC/corporation is the state-law entity; S corp is a federal **tax election** (Form 2553) either can make | always |
| Delaware for every small business by default | Form where you operate unless investors or counsel need Delaware; Delaware + home-state qualification = two sets of fees/filings | always |
| "Writing" requirement wording in Ohio/NC goods contracts | "Record" (electronic counts); hybrid goods-services rule in 2-102 | OH 2026-10-06; NC S.L. 2025-25 |
| Copy-paste online templates as the contract | Draft from the deal facts; list every open business decision for the owner | always |
| NDAs and employee IP agreements without the DTSA notice | Add the 18 U.S.C. §1833(b) immunity notice (or cross-reference a policy) for employees and contractors | 2016-05-11 |
| Paper DMCA agent designation | Online designation, $6, renew every 3 years or it lapses | online-only since 2018 |

## Do this

### Role and framing (every answer)
- Open with jurisdiction, date and posture: "Under [state] law as of [date]...". If the state is unknown, ask or give the NC/OH/DE answer
  plus how to check others.
- Say plainly: research and drafting help, not legal or tax advice; no attorney-client relationship or privilege; a licensed attorney
  should review before signing or filing. Never claim a document was filed, accepted or effective without proof.
- Separate state entity law, federal tax classification, employment law and securities law: one fact (e.g. "LLC with an S election that
  pays a contractor") touches all four.

### Formation workflow (directive 20C: business model -> states -> owners -> entity -> formation -> governance -> licensing -> tax/reporting -> insurance -> agreements -> calendar)
Checklist (directive §13, 15 steps); mark each done, N/A or "owner decision":
1. Facts: where it operates (physically, employees, property), owners, industry, revenue, financing plans, liability exposures.
2. Compare feasible entities (`references/entity-comparison.md`): liability, tax, management, investment, compliance, cost.
3. Current state formation rules and name rules (statute + SOS site, dated).
4. Name availability on the SOS database; separately search USPTO trademarks, state marks and domains. SOS acceptance is not a trademark clearance.
5. Registered agent and office (NC/DE "registered agent"; Ohio "statutory agent"); physical in-state address.
6. Articles/certificate of organization or incorporation.
7. Operating agreement, bylaws, shareholder or buy-sell agreement (`references/contract-clauses.md`).
8. EIN from IRS (free, irs.gov only; one per responsible party per day); state tax registrations.
9. Federal/state tax elections (S election: Form 2553 within 2 months 15 days of the tax year start; Form 8832 for entity classification) with CPA review.
10. Licenses, permits, industry approvals, insurance, employer accounts (withholding, unemployment, workers' comp).
11. Beneficial-ownership and other reporting: FinCEN (US companies exempt as of 2026-08-14) plus any state law; record the check and its date.
12. Compliance calendar: annual reports/taxes (NC Apr 15 for LLCs; DE Mar 1 corps, Jun 1 LLCs), license renewals, tax returns, agent renewals, DMCA agent renewal.
13. Banking, signing authority, separate books, minutes/consents, records kept at the principal office.
14. Foreign qualification in every other state where it "transacts business".
15. Transfer, succession (death/disability), deadlock, dissolution and dispute-resolution terms.

### Entity choice in one screen (full table in references)
- **Sole prop / general partnership**: no filing to exist, unlimited personal liability; a GP can form by conduct (two people splitting profits).
- **LLC**: limited liability, flexible management (member- or manager-managed), default tax: disregarded (1 member) or partnership (2+);
  may elect corporate/S tax. Default for most small businesses.
- **C corp**: needed for venture capital, stock options at scale, many investors; double tax; DE common for VC-backed.
- **S corp**: tax election, max 100 shareholders, one class of stock, no nonresident alien owners; payroll for owner-employees.
- **LP/LLP/LLLP**: funds and professional firms; check state rules on which professions may use LLP/PLLC.
- **Professional entities**: NC Ch. 55B (PC) and G.S. 57D-2-02 (PLLC) need licensing-board involvement; Ohio R.C. 1785 professional associations.
- **Series LLC**: Delaware (6 Del. C. §18-215 protected series, §18-218 registered series), Ohio (R.C. 1706.76); NC: Chapter 57D has no
  series provision (2026-10-10).
- **Benefit corporation**: Delaware public benefit corporation (DGCL subchapter XV) and statutory public benefit LLC; **Ohio** benefit
  corporation = a Chapter 1701 corporation whose articles state a "beneficial purpose" (R.C. 1701.01(FF)-(GG), 1701.96, eff.
  2021-03-24: no duty to beneficiaries, no money damages for not pursuing it); **NC**: no benefit-corporation statute (none in Ch. 55,
  2026-10-10).
- **Nonprofit**: state nonprofit corporation + separate IRS exemption (Form 1023/1023-EZ); exemption is never automatic.

### Contracts (directive 20D: facts -> classification -> governing law -> deal terms -> risk allocation -> mandatory protections -> draft -> negotiation issues -> enforceability review -> consistency check)
- Classify first: goods (UCC Art. 2: NC G.S. Ch. 25, Ohio R.C. Ch. 1302), services/IP (common law), lease (Art. 2A), employment, consumer.
  Mixed deals: predominant purpose, now with the 2022 hybrid-transaction rule in NC/OH.
- Statute of frauds: goods $500+ need a signed record (G.S. 25-2-201; R.C. 1302.04); NC non-competes must be in writing and signed (G.S. 75-4).
- E-signatures: E-SIGN (15 U.S.C. §7001; consumer-consent rules for required disclosures) and UETA (NC G.S. 66-311 et seq.; Ohio R.C. Ch. 1306).
- Arbitration: FAA 9 U.S.C. §2; sexual-harassment/assault disputes cannot be forced into arbitration at the claimant's election (9 U.S.C. §402).
- Indemnity, limitation of liability, liquidated damages, assignment, force majeure, termination/cure, governing law and venue:
  `references/contract-clauses.md` for the enforceability limits and per-contract clause lists.
- Always produce, with the draft: (1) open business decisions for the owner, (2) high-risk or unusual clauses and why, (3) assumptions,
  (4) what a lawyer must check in the governing state.

### Workers
- Decide classification per regime (wage-hour, tax, unemployment, workers' comp, labor law): one worker can be a contractor for one and an
  employee for another. Use `references/worker-classification.md`. Ohio presumes employment for unemployment unless the business shows
  freedom from direction or control (R.C. 4141.01(B)); construction has a 10-of-20-factor presumption in Ohio UC and workers' comp.
- NC workers' comp applies at 3+ regular employees (G.S. 97-2, 97-13(b)); Ohio at 1+ (R.C. 4123.01(B)). Ohio is a state-fund (BWC) state.

### Issue-spotting only (point out, don't resolve)
- **Tax**: federal income/self-employment tax, state income/franchise tax, sales/use tax registration (NC DOR, Ohio Dept. of Taxation),
  payroll. Refer to a CPA.
- **Raising money**: any sale of equity, SAFEs, convertible notes or LLC units is a securities offering. Reg D Rule 506(b) (no general
  solicitation, up to 35 non-accredited sophisticated) vs 506(c) (general solicitation, all accredited, verified; SEC staff no-action
  letter to Latham & Watkins, 2025-03-12, accepts a high minimum investment plus written representations of accredited status and no
  third-party financing as reasonable verification; the $200k individual / $1M entity figures come from the request letter, per
  law-firm reports; staff letters bind nobody). Form D within 15 days after first sale; state notice filings. Refer to a securities lawyer.
- **Trademarks**: state SOS acceptance of a name gives no trademark rights; federal registration at USPTO (Lanham Act, 15 U.S.C. §1051 et
  seq.); Ohio state mark $125 (R.C. 111.16(U)). **Copyright**: register before suing for US works; DMCA agent ($6, 3-year renewal).
  **Patents**: refer to a registered patent practitioner; public disclosure starts clocks.
- **Insurance**: GL, commercial auto (personal policies usually exclude business use), workers' comp, cyber, E&O; "additional insured"
  (covered person) differs from "loss payee" (gets paid for property loss). Contract insurance clauses must match real policies.
- **Leases, bankruptcy, succession**: commercial leases (personal guarantees, use clauses, CAM, assignment); creditor rights and
  UCC-1 filings; buy-sell funding and estate planning for owners. Refer.

### When NOT to add complexity
- Don't recommend Delaware, a holding company, series LLC or S election without a concrete reason (investors, liability segregation, tax
  savings a CPA confirmed). Every added entity is another return, agent, report and bank account.
- Don't add clauses the deal doesn't need; a short, clear contract beats a long boilerplate one nobody reads.

## Security
- **Confidential information**: ask only for facts the task needs. Never request or store SSNs, full EINs, bank/card numbers, passwords
  or ID scans in drafts, notes or repos; use placeholders. Redact before sharing. Don't paste client documents into third-party tools.
- **Veil piercing**: courts disregard the entity when owners treat it as themselves. Prevent: separate bank account, no commingling,
  adequate capitalization and insurance, sign as "Name, Manager of X LLC", keep minutes/consents, keep annual reports and agent current,
  arm's-length documented owner loans. A single-member LLC that pays personal bills is the classic failure.
- **Fraud and illegality**: don't help hide owners from lawful disclosure, structure to evade creditors, backdate documents, misclassify
  workers knowingly, or sell unregistered securities. Flag and stop.
- **Filing scams and solicitation mail**: official notices come from the SOS or your registered agent. NC SOS warns about "priority/early
  annual report" vendors; Delaware warns of deceptive tax and annual-report solicitations; the IRS says an EIN is always free; fake
  "FinCEN BOI compliance" letters and emails demanding fees are common (and US companies now file nothing). Pay only on the .gov site.
- **E-signature hygiene**: verify signer identity and authority (title, entity name), keep the audit trail, never sign for someone.

## Performance & quality
- Every legal statement carries: jurisdiction, source (statute section or agency page), and date checked.
- Fees and forms: give the fee only with "per [official source], [date]"; otherwise "(unverified)". Statutes beat secondary summaries.
- A draft is done when: defined terms are consistent, every cross-reference resolves, party names/entity types match the SOS record,
  signature blocks show capacity, governing law matches the parties' states, and the open-decision list is empty or handed to the owner.
- Run the directive §21 adversarial review: right jurisdiction, authority exists and applies at the relevant time, no overbroad
  remedy, no protected data, would opposing counsel spot an immediate weakness.

## Common mistakes in AI-written/AI-assisted legal work
- Repeating 2024 BOI guidance (30/90-day deadlines, $500/day penalties) to a US company today.
- Treating "S corp" as an entity type; filing "S corp articles". Recommending an S election without payroll/reasonable-compensation math by a CPA.
- Inventing fees, form numbers or deadlines (e.g. "NC annual report $50", "Ohio LLC biennial report"). R.C. 111.16's fee list has no
  annual report for Ohio LLCs or for-profit corporations (ohiosos.gov blocked automated access 2026-10-10: confirm there); LLPs and
  professional associations file biennially ($25); nonprofits file a statement of continued existence every 5 years (R.C. 1702.59).
- Citing cases that were not retrieved and read; quoting holdings from memory. Case names in this skill are limited to ones verified on official pages.
- Saying non-competes are "banned" or "always enforceable"; ignoring the state's statute and blue-pencil/reformation rules.
- One-size indemnity/limitation clauses that cap the drafter's client's own indemnity or exclude the very damages the client cares about.
- Drafting a consumer contract with venue/arbitration/class waiver without checking consumer-protection overlays and state auto-renewal laws.
- Calling someone a contractor because the contract says so: substance controls in every test.
- Forgetting foreign qualification when a Delaware or out-of-state entity actually operates in NC or Ohio.
- Assuming SOS name acceptance = trademark rights, or a domain = trademark.
- Omitting the DTSA notice; using "work made for hire" for contractor software without a backup assignment (software is usually not a WMFH category for contractors).
