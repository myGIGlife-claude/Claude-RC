# Worker classification (as of 2026-10-10)

Classification is decided separately under each regime. Label used in a contract never controls; actual working relationship does.
Tests change with administrations: check the agency page on the day.

## Federal
| Regime | Test | Status 2026-10 |
|---|---|---|
| FLSA (minimum wage, overtime) | Economic reality: is the worker economically dependent on the business or in business for themselves? | 2024 DOL rule (six factors, totality; eff. 2024-03-11) still codified; Field Assistance Bulletin 2025-1 (2025-05-01) stopped WHD from applying it in investigations. NPRM published 2026-02-27 (91 FR 9932): two core factors (control; opportunity for profit/loss) + skill, permanence, integral; also FMLA and MSPA. Comments closed 2026-04-28. No final rule in the Federal Register as of 2026-10-10. Courts use their circuit's test (4th Cir. for NC, 6th Cir. for OH) |
| Federal employment tax (IRS) | Common-law control: behavioral, financial, type of relationship; no single factor decides | IRS page updated 2026-05-19. Form SS-8 determination (6+ months). Section 530 relief (reasonable basis, consistent 1099 filing, consistent treatment). VCSP (Form 8952) to reclassify prospectively with reduced liability |
| NLRA (union rights) | Common-law agency test; entrepreneurial opportunity weighed as one factor (is the worker running an independent business?) | *The Atlanta Opera, Inc.* (Board, 2023-06-13) restored the 2014 *FedEx Home Delivery* approach and overruled *SuperShuttle* (2019) (nlrb.gov news story, 2026-10-10). No later Board decision found as of 2026-10-10 (whether a later Board has revisited it: unverified; check nlrb.gov) |
| Title VII / ADA / ADEA | Common-law agency (control) | Contractors not covered; misclassified employees are |
| ERISA / benefits | Common-law test | Plan eligibility terms matter |

## North Carolina
- Unemployment (Employment Security Law, G.S. Ch. 96): "employee" defined by reference to IRC §3306, i.e. the federal common-law test;
  independent contractors excluded (G.S. 96-1(b)).
- Wage and Hour Act (G.S. Ch. 95, Art. 2A): follows FLSA concepts (unverified detail).
- Workers' comp (G.S. Ch. 97): covers employments with 3+ regular employees (G.S. 97-2, 97-13(b)); NC courts apply a multi-factor common-law
  control test (case law not retrieved this session).
- Employee Fair Classification Act (G.S. 143-785 to 143-791, S.L. 2017-203): Industrial Commission's Employee Classification Section takes
  misclassification reports, investigates and refers to Revenue, DES, Labor; annual report due October 1 (G.S. 143-788).

## Ohio
- Unemployment (R.C. 4141.01(B), eff. 2025-09-30 version): service for pay is employment **unless** the business shows the worker is and
  will stay free from direction or control, both by contract and in fact; ODJFS defines "direction or control" by rule. Construction
  under a construction contract: direction/control presumed if 10 or more of the listed 20 criteria apply (4141.01(B)(2)(k)).
- Workers' comp (R.C. 4123.01(A)(1)(c)): construction-contract workers are employees if at least 10 of 20 listed criteria apply; employers
  with 1+ employees need BWC coverage (4123.01(B)). Ohio is a state-fund (monopolistic) system: private workers' comp policies don't cover Ohio.
- Overtime (R.C. 4111.03, eff. 2022-07-06): mirrors FLSA sections 7 and 13.
- Motor-carrier contractor-driver statute (reported by law firms; section and status unverified this session).

## ABC tests
- An ABC test (worker free from control; work outside the usual course of business; independently established trade) is used by some
  states for wage or unemployment law (e.g. California, Massachusetts, New Jersey). Neither NC nor Ohio uses a general ABC test in the
  statutes checked here. Check the worker's state.

## Consequences of misclassification
- Back wages and overtime (FLSA: plus equal liquidated damages and fees; 2-year or 3-year willful look-back).
- Federal employment taxes, penalties and interest (IRC §3509 reduced rates may apply when not intentional); state withholding.
- Unemployment contributions, interest and penalties; workers' comp premiums and exposure to uninsured-injury claims (Ohio: non-complying
  employer liability; NC G.S. 97-94: $1 per employee per day of non-coverage, min $20 / max $100 a day, Class H felony if willful,
  Class 1 misdemeanor if neglect, personal liability for the person able to bring the employer into compliance).
- Benefits plan eligibility, ACA employer mandate counts, I-9 duties, anti-discrimination coverage.
- Contractual "I am a contractor" waivers do not waive wage rights.

## Practical checklist before calling someone a contractor
1. Do they control how, when and where they work? Own tools and expenses? Can they make or lose money by their own choices?
2. Do they serve other clients and market themselves? Have their own entity, insurance, licenses?
3. Is the work part of your core business, ongoing and indefinite?
4. Is pay per project/invoice rather than hourly with set schedules?
5. Collect W-9; issue 1099-NEC at $2,000+ (tax years beginning after 2025); keep the contract consistent with reality.
6. If in doubt: employee, or ask an employment lawyer; consider SS-8 or VCSP with a CPA.
