# Formation and upkeep: North Carolina, Ohio, Delaware, and any other state (as of 2026-10-10)

Fees are statutory or from the official fee schedule, read on 2026-10-10. Online portals may add card/processing fees. Expedite fees
extra. Re-check before filing: fees and forms change.

- Nature of business is now required on domestic annual reports (Division notice, 2026-10-10).
- Franchise tax trap: the authorized-shares method on a startup with millions of authorized shares produces a huge bill; recalculate
  with the assumed par value capital method before paying (Division's "How to Calculate Franchise Taxes" page).
- DGCL 2025 (SB 21, signed 2025-03-25, 85 Del. Laws c. 6): §144 safe harbors for interested-director/officer and controlling-stockholder
  deals (disinterested committee or disinterested-stockholder approval, or fairness; both approvals for going-private), statutory
  "controlling stockholder" definition (majority voting power, or one-third plus managerial authority); §220 enumerates "books and
  records", requires a good-faith demand with reasonable particularity and allows confidentiality conditions (delcode text, 2026-10-10).

## Foreign qualification (doing business in another state)
- A Delaware (or any out-of-state) entity that **transacts business** in NC or Ohio must qualify there: NC certificate of authority
  ($250 LLC or corp), Ohio foreign LLC registration ($99) or foreign corporation license ($99). Then it pays both states' annual
  obligations (e.g. DE $400 + NC $200 for an LLC) and keeps a registered/statutory agent in each.
- "Transacting business" is defined by each state's statute with safe-harbor lists (isolated transactions, holding meetings, bank
  accounts, interstate commerce). Office, employees or property in the state almost always count.
- Consequences of not qualifying: cannot sue in that state's courts until qualified, penalties and back fees, possible personal
  exposure in some states; contracts usually remain valid.

## Any other state: how to find the rules
1. Official SOS (or Division/Department of Corporations) site: formation page, fee schedule, forms, annual-report rules, scam notices.
2. The state's code on the legislature's official site: LLC act, business corporation act, filing-fee section, registered agent and
   names chapter, foreign-entity chapter.
3. Department of Revenue: business registration, sales tax, withholding, franchise/privilege taxes (e.g. some states tax LLCs flat).
4. Labor/employment agency: unemployment, workers' comp threshold, new-hire reporting.
5. Local: city/county license, zoning, occupancy permits.
6. Record each fact as "per [URL], accessed [date]". If the official site blocks access, say so and mark (unverified).

## Federal steps (all states)
- **EIN**: irs.gov only, free; online Mon-Fri 6 a.m.-1 a.m., Sat 6 a.m.-9 p.m., Sun 6 p.m.-midnight ET; one EIN per responsible party
  per day; responsible party needs SSN/ITIN; form the state entity first (IRS EIN page, updated 2026-08-19).
- **Elections**: Form 2553 (S) within 2 months 15 days of the tax-year start or during the prior year; late relief under Rev. Proc.
  2013-30 within 3 years 75 days (IRS i2553). Form 8832 for check-the-box classification.
- **BOI/CTA**: US-formed entities exempt (final rule, 91 FR 52508, eff. 2026-08-14). Foreign-formed entities registered in a state still
  report (non-US owners and applicants). Check fincen.gov/boi on the day.
- **Payroll**: Form W-4/I-9 for employees, federal deposits, Form 941/940; state new-hire reporting.
- **Information returns**: Form 1099-NEC at $2,000+ for tax years beginning after 2025; collect Form W-9 before paying contractors.
