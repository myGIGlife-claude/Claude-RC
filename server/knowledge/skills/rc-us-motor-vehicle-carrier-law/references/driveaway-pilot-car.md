# Driveaway/towaway, chase cars, pilot/escort vehicles, oversize permits (as of 2026-10-10)

## Driveaway-towaway (federal)
- Definition, 49 CFR 390.5T (390.5 is suspended; identical text): an empty or unladen motor vehicle, with one or more sets of wheels on
  the road, moved (1) between manufacturer facilities, (2) manufacturer to dealer or buyer, (3) dealer/seller/lessor to buyer or lessee,
  (4) to a terminal or shop after disabling crash damage, (5) to a terminal or shop for component failure, or (6) by saddle-mount or
  tow-bar. FMCSA guidance (62 FR 16370, 1997): a mobile home or trailer on its own wheels is driveaway only when it is the commodity;
  stacked trailers are cargo, not driveaway.
- Status of the company: a driveaway company transports property (the vehicle) for compensation in interstate commerce, so it is a
  for-hire motor carrier. **Operating authority does not depend on vehicle weight**: Part 365 / 49 U.S.C. 13902 attach to for-hire
  transport of regulated property, and 387.303T(b)(1) prices the $300,000 filing for fleets of only under-10,001-lb vehicles. Vehicles
  moved on their own wheels are not in the 49 U.S.C. 13506(a) exemption list (read on LII 2026-10-10) or Part 372 commodity lists, so
  interstate for-hire driveaway of cars and light trucks needs authority, BOC-3 and a Part 387 filing even though the FMCSR safety
  rules (DQ file, HOS, Part 396) attach only when the driven vehicle is a 10,001+ lb CMV. No FMCSA interpretation specific to
  light-vehicle driveaway was retrieved (fmcsa.dot.gov blocked): confirm before advising.
- Driver: CMV driven (10,001+ lb) -> DQ file, medical certificate, HOS. CDL if the driven unit or combination meets Part 383, even
  empty (62 FR 16370, 383.3 Q13: empty school bus manufacturer-to-distributor needs a CDL; 383.93 Q3: no passenger endorsement for empty buses).
- ELD: not required when the driven vehicle is part of the shipment, or is a motor home or RV trailer (395.8(a)(1)(ii)(A)(2)-(3)); RODS
  still required unless a short-haul exemption fits.
- Equipment: 393.17 lamps for driveaway combinations; brakes on towed driveaway vehicles have special rules (393.42); saddle-mount and
  tow-bar inspections before and after trips (396.15); 396.11 DVIR excludes driveaway-towaway.
- Marking: 390.21T(f) allows a removable marking device on driven vehicles.
- Cargo claims: the driven vehicle is cargo; Carmack (49 U.S.C. 14706) liability applies to interstate for-hire carriage.

## Chase cars
- A car that follows to bring drivers back. Under 10,001 lb and carrying 8 or fewer people (incl. driver) for compensation -> not a 390.5T
  CMV. Carrying 9-15 for compensation makes it a CMV (passenger). Needs commercial auto insurance; personal policies usually exclude
  business use. This follows from the definition text only; the 1997 guidance (62 FR 16370) has no chase-car Q&A and fmcsa.dot.gov
  was blocked: no agency interpretation retrieved (unverified).

## Pilot/escort vehicle operators (PEVO)
- No federal licence or federal certification. FHWA published a voluntary PEVO best-practices guideline and training curriculum
  (unverified on fhwa.dot.gov this session). Each state sets certification, equipment, insurance, and when escorts are required.
- Reciprocity is a state list, not national. A state that requires certification may accept other states' certificates.
- The permit controls: read the specific oversize/overweight permit (routes, hours, escorts, police escort, height pole, curfews).
- Escort vehicles themselves: usually light vehicles (not CMVs), but the escort business is commercial: commercial auto insurance and
  any state-required liability amount.

### How to find a state's PEVO and OS/OW rules
1. State DOT "oversize/overweight permits" page and the permit manual or permit conditions form.
2. Search the state administrative code for "escort vehicle" and "pilot car".
3. Check certification and reciprocity pages; note certificate expiry and carry requirements.
4. Check the state patrol/commercial vehicle enforcement page for travel-time limits, holiday curfews, flagging rules.
5. Record each source with access date; if inaccessible, say so.

### Ohio (verified 2026-10-10)
- Authority: ODOT special hauling permits; OAC 5501:2-1-04(L) (eff. 2023-11-03): private or police escorts at the director's discretion,
  specified in the permit; escort driver must be a licensed driver able to act as flag person; permittees choosing escorts must tell ODOT.
- No Ohio PEVO certification program in the rule or ODOT forms; OS-1A only requires a licensed driver able to flag (unverified that
  none exists elsewhere).
- ODOT Form OS-1A (DOT-2198A, rev. 01/18; read at haulingpermits.transportation.ohio.gov/OS1A.pdf 2026-10-10) escort conditions:
  - Sign: yellow, black letters "OVERSIZE LOAD", 5 ft x 12 in, 8 in letters; roof-mounted amber flashing or rotating light; radio contact
    with the permitted vehicle; escort driver acts as flagger; may not operate other equipment while escorting.
  - One rear escort if permitted length exceeds 90 ft.
  - One lead (rear on multi-lane highways) escort if width exceeds 13 ft.
  - One lead escort with height-sensing device if height exceeds 14 ft 6 in.
  - Lead and rear escorts if width exceeds 14 ft 6 in or height exceeds 14 ft 10 in; two escorts if two or more of the length/width/height
    triggers apply.
  - Escort vehicle must be a single unit, not towing, with clear front and rear vision.
- Ohio Turnpike issues its own permits (separate authority) (unverified for current limits).

### North Carolina (verified 2026-10-10)
- Authority: G.S. 20-119 (NCDOT special permits; refers to "certified escort vehicle operator"); 19A NCAC 02D .0644 (readopted eff.
  2019-07-01; text read on reports.oah.state.nc.us 2026-10-10).
- 19A NCAC 02D .0644: application to NCDOT with a certified driving record; qualify as (1) an escort certified by another state's
  approved program, (2) an NC law-enforcement officer, or (3) age 21 (18 with a Class A CDL), unrestricted licence, no reckless/DWI
  citation in 12 months, an approved National Safety Council defensive-driving course, and 75 % on the exam after the 8 classroom-hour
  NCDOT course given by the community-college system. Certificate valid **4 years**; revoked for licence loss, reckless/DWI conviction or
  unsafe escort work; 15-day written appeal to the Secretary of Transportation.
- Certification required before escorting OS/OW loads in NC; credentials carried in the vehicle (Publication E-9, rev. 8/24). Reciprocity
  (NCDOT Reciprocation Agreement, rev. 08/19): AZ, CO, FL, GA, MN, OK, PA, UT, VA, WA certificates are accepted; CO does not accept NC's.
- Equipment (E-9, rev. 8/24, read 2026-10-10): single vehicle 2,000-26,000 lb GVWR, no trailer, no hazmat; yellow bumper banner the full
  bumper width (front bumper for lead, rear for trailing escort); amber rotating/strobe light visible 500 ft; headlights on; height pole
  for loads over 14 ft 5 in; two-way radio; stop/slow paddle, safety vest, three reflective triangles, 5 lb fire extinguisher; company
  name and phone sign on each side; no passengers except another certified operator; no electronic devices except hazard communication;
  pull over periodically to release traffic.
- When escorts are required (E-9): front escort over 12 ft wide on two-lane roads (rear on multi-lane or per permit); front height-pole car
  over 14 ft 5 in; front escort over 149,999 lb; three escorts (one NC State Highway Patrol) over 16 ft wide; more as the permit states.
  The length and rear-overhang triggers (previously noted as 110 ft / 150 ft / 15 ft) did not survive PDF text extraction on
  2026-10-10: unverified, read the E-9 PDF.

## Federal size and weight (23 CFR 658, current to 2026-10-07)
- Width: 102 in on the National Network (Hawaii 108 in); states may permit wider loads (658.15(c)).
- Weight on the Interstate: 80,000 lb gross (bridge formula may lower), 20,000 lb single axle, 34,000 lb tandem (658.17).
- Length: states may not set less than 48 ft for a semitrailer in a tractor-semitrailer, 28 ft for doubles trailers (658.13).
- Height is set by states: Part 658 contains no height provision (full text of 658.13, .15, .17 checked 2026-10-10); check each state.
