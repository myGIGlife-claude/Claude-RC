## Currency check
Verified 2026-10-10 unless marked.
- **eCFR Title 49** current through 2026-10-07 (last amended 2026-10-05). **Title 23** (FHWA size/weight) through 2026-10-07.
- **Suspended "T" sections are the operative text.** 49 CFR 390.3 and **390.5 are both suspended** (390.5 since 2017, re-suspended
  2021-07-07); **390.3T** and **390.5T** apply (the 390.5T definitions quoted below are word-for-word the same as 390.5; state rules
  still say "390.5"). 390.201 (URS registration) was suspended indefinitely 2023-11-17; the operative registration rules are **390.19T**
  (MCS-150 before operating and every 24 months) and **390.200T** (new-applicant *private* and *exempt for-hire* carriers file Form
  MCSA-1; non-exempt for-hire applicants file the OP-1 series with the MCS-150, 390.19T(e), now inside Motus). Cite the T section.
- **Motus**, FMCSA's registration system (notice 91 FR 23144, 2026-04-29): Phase I 2025-12-08 (BOC-3 filers, insurers, service
  providers); Phase II for all registrants **launched 2026-05-19** (fmcsa.dot.gov "Move to Motus", read via Wayback). Because of
  rollout problems FMCSA **temporarily suspended USDOT inactivation** for biennial updates due since 2026-06-01, "until further notice"
  (fmcsa.dot.gov page updated 2026-06-22, Wayback 2026-07-15): the update is still owed. FMCSA says it has *proposed* no longer assigning
  MC numbers (USDOT number plus suffixes as sole identifier) and that this needs an NPRM and is not in the first Motus release; no NPRM
  found on federalregister.gov as of 2026-10-10. MC numbers still exist and are still required on freight documents.
- **Broker/freight-forwarder security: $75,000** surety bond (BMC-84) or trust fund (BMC-85); the 2023 rule (88 FR 78656, 2023-11-16)
  became fully enforceable **2026-01-16** (compliance date moved by 89 FR 107021): trust assets limited to cash, irrevocable letters of
  credit, Treasury bonds; surety/trustee must notify FMCSA of payments that drop the fund below $75,000; FMCSA suspends broker authority
  7 business days after its notice unless the fund is restored (49 CFR 387.307(e)).
- **Non-domiciled CDL final rule** 91 FR 7044 (2026-02-13), effective **2026-03-16**: non-domiciled CLP/CDL limited to holders of specific
  employment-based nonimmigrant status; reaffirms the 2025-09-29 interim final rule (90 FR 46509), which the D.C. Circuit had stayed
  on 2025-11-10/13 (*Lujan v. FMCSA*, No. 25-1215; stated in the final rule preamble). A new petition against the final rule
  (reported as No. 26-1032, stay denied 2026-05-05, expedited briefing) is trade-press only: **unverified**, check the docket.
- **English proficiency**: 391.11(b)(2) requires reading/speaking English sufficiently. NPRM 91 FR 51422 (2026-08-10, comments closed
  2026-10-09) proposes codifying ELP as an out-of-service violation, matching CVSA OOS criteria already enforced. Not final.
- **Speed limiters**: FMCSA/NHTSA withdrew the 2016 NPRM and 2022 ANSPRM on 2025-07-24. No federal speed-limiter mandate.
- **Broker transparency** (Part 371.3 records access): NPRM 2024-11-20, comment reopened to 2025-03-20; no final rule as of 2026-10-10.
- **2026 deregulatory final rules**: eDVIRs expressly allowed (eff. 2026-03-23); ELD manual no longer required in the cab (eff. 2026-07-22);
  CDL holders no longer self-report violations to their state (eff. 2026-07-22); emergency-exemption clarification (2026-10-05);
  UCR fees up ~20% for registration year 2027 (eff. 2026-10-01).
- **Minimum insurance unchanged**: $750,000 for-hire interstate property, 10,001+ lb GVWR; $300,000 if the fleet is only vehicles under
  10,001 lb (387.9; 387.303T). 387.9 was technically amended 2026-07-21 (91 FR 45660) without changing amounts.
- **DOL independent-contractor rule**: NPRM 2026-02-27 proposes rescinding the 2024 rule (29 CFR part 795) and returning to the 2021
  "core factors" test; comments closed 2026-04-28; no final rule found as of 2026-10-10. The 2024 rule text is still in the CFR.
- **State changes**: NC minimum auto liability rose to **50/100/50** for policies issued or renewed on/after **2025-07-01** (G.S.
  20-279.21(b)(2); S.L. 2023-133 s. 12, date moved from 2025-01-01 by S.L. 2024-29 s. 8(c)). Ohio stays 25/50/25 (R.C. 4509.51).
  Ohio PUCO incorporates 49 CFR as of **2025-08-01** (OAC 4901:2-5-02(C), eff. 2026-01-01). Ohio HB 195 (136th GA) amended UCC Chapter
  1310 (Article 2A) and others effective **2026-10-06** (2022 UCC amendments). NC enacted the 2022 UCC amendments (new Article 12,
  conforming Article 2A/9 changes) by S.L. 2025-25 Part VIII, effective **2025-10-01**.

## What changed / stop doing
| Old advice | Do instead | Since |
|---|---|---|
| "A 26-foot box truck needs a CDL" / "under 26 ft is non-CDL" | Length decides nothing. Read the door-jamb GVWR (and GCWR if towing); CDL turns on 26,001 lb rating/weight, passengers 16+, placarded hazmat | always |
| "Under 26,001 lb, no FMCSA rules" | Interstate CMV starts at **10,001 lb** (390.5): USDOT number, med card, DQ file, HOS/ELD, Part 393/396, insurance | always |
| Cite 49 CFR 390.3 / 390.5 / 390.201 | Cite 390.3T, 390.5T, 390.19T, 390.200T (the suspended sections are not operative) | 2017 / 2023-11-17 |
| "Get an MC number first" as a fixed rule | Interstate for-hire still needs operating authority (Part 365) and gets a docket (MC) number; apply in Motus since 2026-05-19; MC-number retirement only proposed | 2026-05-19 |
| "Missed biennial update = instant deactivation" | Still owed (390.19T(b)(4)), but inactivation for updates due since 2026-06-01 is paused during the Motus rollout | 2026-06 |
| "Broker bond $75k, any asset in trust" | Cash, irrevocable LOC or Treasury bonds only; draw-down triggers suspension | 2026-01-16 |
| "Speed limiters are coming" | Withdrawn | 2025-07-24 |
| "Keep the ELD manual in the cab" | Not required; driver must still know how to operate and transfer data | 2026-07-22 |
| "NC minimum is 30/60/25" | 50/100/50 for policies on/after 2025-07-01 | 2025-07-01 |
| "DOL 2024 six-factor IC test governs" | 2024 rule still codified, but rescission proposed (2026-02-27); check status, and state tests separately | 2026-02 |

## Do this

### 1. Classification procedure (run it before any answer)
Facts to collect: vehicle(s) and VIN, **GVWR from the FMVSS label**, GCWR if towing, towed unit's GVWR, actual loaded weight, seating,
cargo (hazmat? placards?), for-hire or own goods, origin/destination (state lines, or part of an interstate journey), owner, driver,
who dispatches, dates.
1. **Weight basis.** 390.5T uses GVWR/GCWR *or* actual GVW/GCW, "whichever is greater". A truck rated 9,999 lb loaded to 10,500 lb is a
   CMV. GCWR = manufacturer value on the power unit's FMVSS label, or the sum of unit ratings/weights, whichever is highest; the power
   unit's GCWR is ignored when it is not towing.
2. **Interstate?** Crossing a state line, or an intrastate leg of a movement that originates/terminates out of state (390.5T). If no,
   go to the state's adoption of the FMCSRs (step 6).
3. **FMCSR CMV (390.5T)**: 10,001+ lb; or designed/used for 9+ passengers (incl. driver) for compensation; or 16+ not for compensation;
   or placarded hazmat. Exceptions in 390.3T(f) (government, occasional personal non-commercial moves, school buses, etc.).
4. **CDL (Part 383, applies interstate and intrastate per 390.3T(b))**: Group A 26,001+ lb GCWR with towed unit over 10,000 lb;
   Group B single vehicle 26,001+ lb GVWR; Group C designed for 16+ (incl. driver) or placarded hazmat/select agents.
   Non-CDL box truck = 10,001-26,000 lb, not towing a 10,001+ lb trailer at a 26,001+ combination, no placards.
5. **What applies to an interstate non-CDL CMV (10,001-26,000 lb)**: USDOT number + MCS-150 biennial update (390.19T); marking (390.21T);
   driver qualification file and medical certificate (391.41, 391.51); English proficiency (391.11(b)(2)); HOS (Part 395) with the
   non-CDL short-haul exemption (395.1(e)(2): 150 air-miles, return to reporting location, 14 h on 5 days / 16 h on 2 days in 7, time records
   6 months) or the 150-air-mile short-haul (395.1(e)(1)); ELD unless exempt (395.8(a)(1)(ii): RODS on 8 or fewer days in 30, certain
   driveaway, pre-2000 engines); Part 393 parts, Part 393 subpart I cargo securement; Part 396 inspection/maintenance and annual
   inspection (396.17); for-hire: operating authority + BOC-3 + insurance filing (Parts 365, 387). **Not**: Part 382 drug/alcohol testing,
   Clearinghouse, CDL. Part 382 and the Clearinghouse attach when a CDL is required.
6. **Intrastate**: find the state adoption. Ohio: for-hire intrastate uses the 390.5 (10,001 lb) definition, private carriers the 383.5
   (26,001 lb) one (OAC 4901:2-5-01(B), -03). NC: intrastate motor carrier = 26,001+ lb, 16+ passengers or placarded hazmat (G.S.
   20-376(5); 14B NCAC 07C .0101(b)), but Part 396.17-.23 periodic inspection applies intrastate from **10,001 lb GVWR** (07C .0101(d)).
7. **Authority**: for-hire interstate carrier of regulated property needs authority (49 U.S.C. 13902, Part 365) **whatever the vehicle
   weight** (387.303T(b)(1) sets $300,000 for fleets of only under-10,001-lb vehicles) unless an exemption in 49 U.S.C. 13506 (read on
   LII 2026-10-10; uscode.house.gov under maintenance) or Part 372 fits; brokers need broker authority and the $75,000 security
   (Part 371, 387.307).
8. **Write the answer** with the classification table first (weight basis, interstate yes/no, CMV yes/no, CDL group or none, for-hire
   or private), then obligations, then what to verify. See `references/fmcsa-thresholds.md`.

### 2. Registration and authority (federal)
- **USDOT number**: interstate CMV operators (including private carriers), brokers, forwarders, and intrastate hazmat-permit carriers.
  FMCSA's "Do I need a USDOT number" page (updated 2025-09-03, read via Wayback 2026-10-10) lists **Ohio and North Carolina** among the
  states that require intrastate CMV registrants to hold one; confirm the state's own threshold with PUCO / NC DMV.
- **Biennial MCS-150 update**: month by last digit of USDOT number (1=Jan ... 9=Sep, 0=Oct); year parity by next-to-last digit
  (odd digit = odd year). Missing it risks deactivation (390.19T(b)(4)), paused during the Motus rollout (see Currency).
- **Operating authority** (Part 365) for for-hire interstate carriers of regulated property, passengers, brokers, freight forwarders.
  Then **BOC-3** process agents, **insurance filing** (BMC-91/91X for carriers), **UCR** annual fee, IRP/IFTA when a qualified motor vehicle
  crosses states (IRP/IFTA thresholds are state-administered; unverified here).
- **Who is who**: motor carrier = takes legal responsibility to transport. Broker (371.2) = arranges transport by an authorized carrier
  for compensation; a carrier is not a broker for loads it has accepted and is legally bound to haul. Freight forwarder = assembles and
  consolidates, takes responsibility, uses carriers (Part 387 subpart E). Part 374 is passenger-carrier conduct, not forwarders.
- **Insurance (Part 387)**: public liability is per vehicle class/commodity: see `references/fmcsa-thresholds.md`. Federal cargo security
  is required for household goods carriers (387.303T(c)), not general freight; shippers and brokers often demand it by contract.

### 3. Carmack (49 U.S.C. 14706) in outline
Receiving carrier, delivering carrier and the carrier on whose line the loss occurred are liable for actual loss or injury to property.
Carriers may limit liability to a declared or agreed value if reasonable and offered in writing. Claim period may not be shorter than
9 months; suit period not shorter than 2 years from written disallowance. Broker liability is not set by 14706 (check FAAAA preemption
case law in the circuit; no case cited here). Verified on LII 2026-10-10 (uscode.house.gov down).

### 4. Driveaway and pilot cars
Short form here; full detail and state lookup method in `references/driveaway-pilot-car.md`.
- **Driveaway-towaway operation** (390.5T): an empty/unladen vehicle with wheels on the road moved between manufacturer, dealer, lessor,
  buyer/lessee, to repair after a crash or breakdown, or by saddle-mount/tow-bar. The driven vehicle is the "cargo".
- The driven vehicle is a CMV if 10,001+ lb (rating or weight) in interstate commerce, so the driver needs DQ file and med card, and the
  company USDOT/authority/insurance. ELD exempt when the driven vehicle is part of the shipment or is a motor home/RV trailer
  (395.8(a)(1)(ii)(A)). 396.15(a) drops 396.3 records, the 396.11 DVIR and the 396.17 periodic inspection for a vehicle that is part
  of the shipment; 396.11(a)(5) also excludes driveaway-towaway and one-CMV carriers from the DVIR. Driver of an empty 26,001+ lb
  truck or 16+ seat bus still needs the CDL (FMCSA guidance 62 FR 16370: 383.3 Q13; passenger endorsement not needed for empty buses, 383.93 Q3).
- **Chase car** (returns drivers): not a 390.5T CMV if under 10,001 lb and not carrying 9+ people for compensation (definition text);
  it is still a commercial auto needing commercial insurance. No FMCSA interpretation on chase cars found (62 FR 16370 has none;
  fmcsa.dot.gov blocked): unverified beyond the definition itself.
- **Pilot/escort vehicles**: no federal licence. States set certification, equipment and when escorts are needed, inside each oversize
  permit. Ohio: no state certification program; escort conditions in ODOT Form OS-1A (rev. 01/18, verified 2026-10-10) and OAC
  5501:2-1-04(L). NC: NCDOT certification required (G.S. 20-119; 19A NCAC 02D .0644, readopted eff. 2019-07-01: 8-hour course, 75 %
  exam, certificate 4 years), reciprocity with AZ, CO, FL, GA, MN, OK, PA, UT, VA, WA (NCDOT list rev. 08/19); equipment in
  Publication E-9 (rev. 8/24).
- **Federal size/weight floor** (23 CFR 658): 102 in width on the National Network; 80,000 lb gross, 20,000 single axle, 34,000 tandem
  (bridge formula); states may permit oversize (658.15(c)). Part 658 sets no height limit: height is state law.

### 5. Personal vehicles (Ohio, NC) in outline
Title/lien perfection, salvage, suspensions, tickets, insurance, lemon law, towing: `references/state-notes-oh-nc.md`. Core points:
- Lien on a titled vehicle is perfected by notation on the title, not a UCC filing (Ohio R.C. 4505.13; NC G.S. 20-58 et seq., perfected
  from agreement date if application reaches DMV within 20 days, G.S. 20-58.2). Dealer inventory is the exception (UCC filing).
- Lemon laws exclude most trucks: NC excludes vehicles over 10,000 lb (G.S. 20-351.1); Ohio covers passenger cars and noncommercial
  vehicles (R.C. 1345.71). A box truck buyer usually relies on UCC warranty and Magnuson-Moss instead.

### 6. Fleet leasing and lease-to-own (summary; clause list in `references/leasing-ucc.md`)
- **First question: true lease or disguised security interest?** UCC 1-203 (Ohio R.C. 1301.203; NC G.S. 25-1-203). A non-cancellable
  term plus any bright-line factor (term >= economic life; bound to buy or renew; nominal purchase or renewal option) = security interest.
  Most "lease-to-own" with a $1 or nominal buyout is a **sale with a security interest**: Article 9 governs default, repossession,
  notice and disposition, and the lessor must perfect by title notation, or lose to a bankruptcy trustee.
- **True lease**: Article 2A (Ohio Ch. 1310; NC Ch. 25 Art. 2A). Liquidated damages only at a reasonable amount or formula
  (2A-504: Ohio 1310.50, NC 25-2A-504).
- **Repossession**: self-help only without breach of the peace (9-609: Ohio 1309.609, NC 25-9-609); otherwise judicial process. Then
  notice and commercially reasonable disposition (Article 9 Part 6). Consumer transactions add state notice and deficiency rules
  (Ohio R.C. 1317.12: default-and-cure notice within 5 business days after taking possession in a consumer retail installment sale).
- **Part 376** applies when equipment is leased to an **authorized carrier** for regulated transport. Leases of equipment **without
  driver** from a person principally in the leasing business are exempt except identification (376.21(c)). Leases **with driver**
  (owner-operator) must contain 376.12 items: parties, duration, exclusive possession and complete responsibility of the carrier,
  compensation, payment within 15 days, freight bill access, charge-backs, no forced purchases, insurance, escrow rules, copies.
- **Rental/lease vicarious liability**: Graves Amendment (49 U.S.C. 30106) shields a lessor in the leasing business for the lessee's
  use, absent its own negligence or crime; state financial-responsibility laws survive.

### 7. Contractors, owner-operators, gig drivers
- Status differs by regime: FLSA (federal rule in flux, see Currency), IRS common law, state wage law, unemployment, workers' comp.
  Run each separately. 376.12(c)(4): Part 376 control language does not by itself decide employee status.
- Arbitration: FAA §1 excludes transportation workers' "contracts of employment", which include independent-contractor agreements, and
  a court decides §1 before compelling arbitration: *New Prime Inc. v. Oliveira*, No. 17-340 (U.S. Jan. 15, 2019) (verified 2026-10-10,
  slip opinion). State arbitration law may still apply.
- Rideshare/delivery: Ohio TNCs under R.C. Chapter 4925 (permits, company duties, driver qualifications); NC TNCs under G.S. 20-280.1
  et seq., with insurance in G.S. 20-280.4. Delivery apps using personal cars are usually not CMVs (under 10,001 lb), but personal auto
  policies often exclude delivery: check the endorsement.

### 8. State-by-state method (new state, new question)
1. Statute: the legislature's official code site (search "<state> revised statutes"); never a commercial mirror as sole source.
2. Agency rule: DMV/BMV (title, registration, license), state DOT permit office (OS/OW, escorts), state patrol motor carrier unit
   (FMCSR adoption, inspections), PUC/utilities commission (intrastate for-hire, household goods, towing in some states).
3. FMCSR adoption: look for "adopts 49 CFR" in the safety rule and note the incorporated CFR date and the intrastate weight threshold.
4. Pilot cars: state permit manual + escort certification page; check reciprocity lists; read the permit itself (it controls).
5. Record: URL, section, effective date, date accessed. If a site blocks you, say so and mark (unverified).

## Security
- **Location/GPS data**: telematics creates sensitive location records. Collect only what the lease or safety program needs, disclose it
  in a GPS/privacy addendum the lessee signs, limit access, set a retention period, and don't use it to track a person off duty.
  State privacy and anti-stalking laws apply (unverified per state). Never put live locations or VINs tied to names in shared docs.
- **Remote disablement (starter interrupt)**: high risk. Disabling a moving vehicle is a safety hazard; disable only when parked and
  after any notice the contract and state law require. Disablement can be treated as a repossession or breach of peace; consumer-law
  limits vary by state. No Ohio or NC statute specific to commercial starter interrupts was found (unverified): research before drafting.
- **Repossession safety**: no confrontation, no police "assist" used to intimidate (risk of state action/breach of peace), stop if the
  debtor objects on site, inventory and return personal property, document condition.
- **Fraud**: chameleon carriers (new USDOT/authority to escape a bad record: check officers, addresses, phone, equipment vs old entities);
  double brokering (a carrier re-brokers a load without consent: verify the hauling carrier's USDOT on the rate confirmation matches the
  truck and the payee); identity theft of authority (changed contact info in FMCSA records, fake insurance certificates: confirm insurance
  with the insurer, not the PDF); broker bond claims via the surety (387.307); fake "FMCSA compliance" calls demanding payment for
  MCS-150 updates (the update itself is free).
- **Client data**: CDL numbers, medical certificates, drug-test results (Part 40 confidentiality), DOB and SSNs: minimize and redact.

- Every answer: classification table, then rule cites with section and date, then a "verify" list. Target zero uncited numbers.
- Every dollar amount, weight and date ties to an eCFR, Federal Register, state code or agency page accessed on a stated date.

## Common mistakes in AI-written/AI-assisted legal work
- Using vehicle length ("26-foot box truck") to decide CDL or FMCSR coverage.
- Treating 26,001 lb as the FMCSR threshold (it is the CDL threshold; FMCSRs start at 10,001 lb interstate).
- Ignoring "whichever is greater": actual loaded weight can push a lighter-rated truck into CMV status.
- Applying Part 382 drug testing and the Clearinghouse to non-CDL drivers, or omitting it for CDL drivers in intrastate work.
- Citing suspended 390.3, 390.5, 390.201 or 387.403 instead of the T sections; citing a 2024 DOL rule as settled.
- Saying "no MC number needed anymore" as final law (only proposed as of 2026-10-10), or "no authority needed under 10,001 lb"
  (authority turns on for-hire regulated transport, not weight).
- Calling a $1-buyout "lease" a true lease; forgetting title-notation perfection, so the lessor is unsecured in bankruptcy.
- Drafting late fees or acceleration that operate as penalties; unlimited indemnity for the lessor's own negligence (state anti-indemnity
  and public-policy limits: unverified per state); waiver of breach-of-peace limits (non-waivable under 9-602; Ohio R.C. 1309.602).
- Telling a pilot-car operator a "national certification" exists. Certification is state by state.
- Inventing cases or holdings, or citing trade-press summaries as law. Name a case only if its text was retrieved and checked.
- Promising a ticket will be dismissed or that filing a request stops a suspension.
