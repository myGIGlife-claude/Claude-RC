### Older versions (why the date matters)
- Chapters 3119/3121/3123/3125 first appear with **SB 180 (123rd GA), eff. 2001-03-22**. Before that, support law sat mainly in Chapter 3113
  (R.C. 3119.01 itself still refers to "division (B) of former section 3113.21") and in Chapter 3111. Exact old-to-new mapping: (unverified),
  pull the SB 180 text.
- Default/hearing sections took their current shape in **HB 657 (124th GA), eff. 2002-12-13**; 20% arrears presumption and paternity relief
  in **HB 208, eff. 2002-01-25**; guideline overhaul **HB 366 / SB 70 (132nd GA), eff. 2019-03-28 / 2019-02-11**; health-care rules
  **HB 166, eff. 2019-10-17**.
- Ohio UIFSA: original act effective about **1998-01-01** (old code had a section on "orders issued prior to 1-1-98"; enacting bill
  (unverified)); before it, Chapter 3115 was URESA. UIFSA 2008 version **eff. 2016-01-01 (HB 64, 131st GA)**.
- Service rules changed repeatedly (certified mail only before 1997-07-01; commercial carrier from 2012-07-01; waiver Civ.R. 4.7 from
  2020-07-01). Use the version in force on the service date: `references/historical-law-timeline.md`.

## Do this
**Workflow (owner's directive 20B):** original order -> historical law and service record -> current enforcement status -> payment/arrears
accounting -> issuing and enforcing states -> UIFSA and FFCCSOA -> available tribunal and remedy -> deadline -> evidence -> draft -> review.

1. **Pin the facts from documents, not memory.** Court, case type (divorce, juvenile/parentage, administrative CSEA order), entry date of every
   order, the docket, summons, every return of service (certified-mail green card, sheriff return, publication affidavit, certificate of
   mailing), magistrate decisions, journal entries, and whether notice of the judgment was served under Civ.R. 58(B).
2. **Classify the order.** Court vs administrative (R.C. 3111.81; objection window 14 days, 3111.84; order stays in effect during an
   objection unless stayed). Who issued it decides who can vacate it.
3. **Apply then-law and now-law separately.** Service validity = rules in force on the service date. Today's remedy = today's rules and
   statutes. Write both into the analysis with their effective dates.
4. **Separate five questions** (directive section 5): (a) subject-matter jurisdiction; (b) personal jurisdiction/service; (c) validity of
   the judgment; (d) amount (calculation, credits, ledger); (e) enforcement (withholding, license, offset, interstate). Each has its own
   remedy and its own court.
5. **Choose the remedy that matches the defect** (`references/post-judgment-relief.md`):
   - Defective or no service on a party who never appeared -> common-law motion to vacate as void for lack of personal jurisdiction
     (inherent power, Patton; no 60(B) time limit), supported by a SIGNED, sworn affidavit and the service record. Do not assume: a
     service defect does not by itself make an order void; the court decides whether service was valid and whether the movant's
     evidence outweighs the presumption of regularity. Eighth District illustrations (persuasive only, verified 2026-10-10): a sworn
     denial of residence at the service address shifts the burden to the plaintiff, but an unsigned affidavit has no evidentiary
     value and the 2002 default judgment stood (Margiman v. Dowdell, 2025-Ohio-377); no signed carrier receipt plus a sworn denial
     meant service could not be presumed and the default judgment was void (One Main Fin. Group v. Knight, 2026-Ohio-1660).
   - Party who had notice and appeared without raising service -> likely waived (Lundeen I, 2021-Ohio-1533, verified 2026-10-10).
   - Mistake, fraud, new evidence -> Civ.R. 60(B)(1)-(3): one-year outer limit and reasonable time. 60(B)(4)-(5): reasonable time only.
   - Error in calculation or law by a court with jurisdiction -> voidable: appeal (App.R. 4, 30 days) or objection to magistrate (Civ.R. 53 /
     Juv.R. 40, 14 days). Too late = res judicata, not "void".
   - Wrong default/arrears amount in an enforcement notice -> administrative mistake-of-fact hearing (request within 14 days of the default
     notice, R.C. 3123.04), then court hearing (motion within 14 days, 3123.05).
   - Not the father -> R.C. 3119.961-.962 (genetic test within 6 months before the motion, 0% probability), notwithstanding Civ.R. 60(B).
     Arrears cancellation is discretionary (3119.964(B), "may" cancel).
6. **Say what the remedy reaches.** Vacating a void order removes the order and the arrears built on it as to that party, but it does
   not end the duty to support the child: the obligee or CSEA can seek a new order, and in a parentage case the court may order support
   for the period before the new order, subject to the R.C. 3111.13(F)(2)-(3) limits (verified 2026-10-10). Terminating current support
   does NOT erase arrears (R.C. 3121.36, 3123.14); a modification reaches back only to the date notice of the motion was given
   (3119.83-.84; 42 U.S.C. 666(a)(9)). Refunds of money already collected need a separate request or action (procedure unverified; ask
   the CSEA and court), and whether a refund is available at all is unsettled: never promise one.
7. **Interstate**: build the jurisdiction matrix in `references/ohio-nc-interstate.md` before drafting anything for NC.
8. **Deadlines**: compute every period from the rule text, note the trigger event, and list what does and does not toll it
   (a 60(B) motion does NOT extend App.R. 4 time; objections under Civ.R. 53(D)(3)(b) do).
9. **Least dependency**: work from the official code, rules PDF and court docket. No paid database is required for this skill; don't
   recommend a service, app or "document preparer" when the court's own forms or the CSEA can do it.

**When a licensed lawyer is strongly advised (say so plainly):** any void-judgment or 60(B) motion; anything near a deadline; contempt or
criminal nonsupport exposure (jail risk); NC registration contests (20-day clock); paternity disestablishment; large arrears or
license/passport loss; domestic violence or safety concerns; any appeal. Point to (all reached 2026-10-10): Ohio Legal Help
(https://www.ohiolegalhelp.org/, legal aid finder), the county CSEA via the state portal (https://childsupport.ohio.gov/), Legal Aid of
North Carolina (https://legalaidnc.org/), NC Child Support Services (https://www.ncdhhs.gov/divisions/social-services/child-support-services).

## Security
- **Children's and parties' data**: never put a child's full name, birth date, SSN, account numbers, employer IDs or addresses in drafts
  meant for filing. Sup.R. 11.13 (formerly 45(D)): the filer must omit personal identifiers (SSN except last four, financial account
  numbers, employer and employee ID numbers; juvenile's name in abuse/neglect/dependency cases) and file them on a separate form. The clerk
  will not catch it for you. Use initials and "last four" in work product too.
- **IV-D confidentiality**: R.C. 3125.50 bars disclosure of IV-D applicant/recipient information except as rules allow; 42 U.S.C. 654(26)
  requires safeguards, including withholding a party's or child's whereabouts where a protective order exists. Never help locate a party
  for someone who may be under a protection order; route location requests through the CSEA.
- **Sealed and restricted records**: don't paste sealed documents, genetic test results or medical records into outputs. If a user supplies
  them, summarize only what the task needs.
- **Payment scams**: Ohio payments go to the Office of Child Support as trustee (R.C. 3121.44); money paid directly to the other parent is
  deemed a gift unless shown to discharge another obligation (3121.45). Treat any request to pay arrears by gift card, crypto, wire to a
  person, or to a "settlement agent" as a likely scam; verify with the county CSEA using a number from the county's website, not from the
  message.
- **What not to file**: no unredacted exhibits, no full ledgers with SSNs, no children's school or medical records unless the court requires
  them and then under the court's confidentiality procedure, no allegations of fraud without evidence (Civ.R. 11 risk). Never claim a
  document was filed, served or granted without a stamped copy or docket entry.
- Public repo hygiene: no real names or case numbers in this skill or in examples.

## Performance & quality (research quality bar)
- Every statute/rule cite carries: section, effective date of the version used, URL, date checked. Every case: court, citation, year,
  pinpoint, proposition, and how verified.
- Primary sources first: codes.ohio.gov (current + "Available Versions" with authenticated PDFs), Supreme Court of Ohio rules PDFs (they
  include staff notes with amendment history) and opinions (supremecourt.ohio.gov/rod/docs), ncleg.gov, govinfo/uscode for federal.
- Precedential weight: Supreme Court of Ohio > own appellate district > other districts (persuasive). Say which district the case is in.
- Quotations at most 15 words; paraphrase otherwise.
- A draft is not "filing-ready" until captions, case number placeholders, service certificate, local-rule formatting and redaction are
  checked against the specific court's local rules (unverified per court; read them).

## Common mistakes in AI-written/AI-assisted legal work
- Inventing or "remembering" cases, pinpoints or quotations; citing GTE or Patton without checking the syllabus paragraph.
- Calling an order "void" because the amount was wrong or a rule was misapplied (that is voidable: Harper, Ostanek).
- Saying lack of notice proves defective service. The record controls: a signed certified-mail receipt by any person at the address,
  residence service, refused mail followed by ordinary mail (Civ.R. 4.6(C)), or publication with a diligence affidavit may be valid service
  even if the person says they never knew. Analyze the return, don't assume.
- Using today's Civ.R. 4.1 (commercial carrier, waiver) to judge 1990s service, or today's 3119.861 to judge when a 2010 order ended.
- Treating termination of current support as ending arrears, or a modification as reaching back before notice.
- Saying a motion, objection or appeal stops withholding, offsets or license action.
- Telling someone to "file in North Carolina to vacate the Ohio order": NC can refuse to enforce on a 52C-6-607 defense but cannot vacate or
  modify an Ohio order except under 52C-6-611/613 conditions; the validity attack on the order itself goes to the Ohio issuing court.
- Missing the 20-day window to contest NC registration (52C-6-605/606) and the preclusion that follows (52C-6-608).
- Applying NC interest or NC duration rules to an Ohio order (issuing-state law governs, 52C-6-604; 1738B(h)).
- Citing Sup.R. 45 in a 2026-07-01+ filing; citing "R.C. 3113.21" as current law.
- Promising outcomes, or implying Claude is a lawyer or that a conversation is privileged.
