# Ohio order, North Carolina collection (as of 2026-10-10)

Statutes read 2026-10-10: 28 U.S.C. 1738B (govinfo, 2023 ed.), R.C. Chapter 3115 (codes.ohio.gov), N.C. Gen. Stat. Chapter 52C (ncleg.gov),
NC CSS policy manual, Intergovernmental chapter (policies.ncdhhs.gov/wp-content/uploads/Intergovernmental_ADA-Accessible.pdf, no revision
date on the PDF). Ohio and NC both run UIFSA 2008 (Ohio since 2016-01-01, NC since 2015-06-24), so section numbers match:
R.C. 3115.xyz <-> G.S. 52C-x-xyz (parallel texts compared 2026-10-10 for 201, 205-207, 315/316, 501-507, 601-608, 611, 613).

## FFCCSOA, 28 U.S.C. 1738B (key points)
- (a) Every state must enforce, by its terms, an order "made consistently with this section", and may modify only under (e), (f), (i).
- (c) "Consistently" = issuing court had subject-matter jurisdiction AND personal jurisdiction over the contestants, AND gave reasonable
  notice and opportunity to be heard. A disputed-service case attacks exactly this element.
- (d) Continuing, exclusive jurisdiction (CEJ) stays with the issuing state while it is the child's state or the residence of any individual
  contestant, or the parties consented in a record or open court (consent language enacted 2014-09-29, in force 2017-01-01).
- (e) Another state may modify only if it has jurisdiction under (i) and the issuing state lost CEJ, or all individual contestants filed
  written consent with the CEJ state.
- (g) A state that lost CEJ may still enforce non-modifiable and accrued obligations.
- (h) Forum law generally; issuing state's law for interpreting the order including duration; longer statute of limitation of forum vs
  issuing state for arrears.

## How NC enforces a registered Ohio order
- Registration: letter of transmittal, two copies of the order (one certified), sworn or certified arrears statement, obligor data
  (52C-6-602). NC Child Support Services sends a Notice of Registration of Foreign Order (DSS-4528, in NC's ACTS system since 2020-01-27);
  the manual recommends N.C. R. Civ. P. 4 service, counts the 20 days from service of the notice, and tells caseworkers to allow 3 extra
  days under N.C. R. Civ. P. 6(e) (manual read 2026-10-10). The statute itself says "within 20 days after notice" (52C-6-605(b)(2)):
  compute from the earliest plausible date and do not rely on the 3-day allowance.
- After confirmation: NC procedures and remedies (income withholding, other NC enforcement) apply; Ohio law governs amount, duration and
  arrears/interest (52C-6-604). NC policy: NC has no interest statute for child support but collects interest when the issuing state's law
  allows and the initiating state computes it (NCDHHS manual). Ohio interest exists only where an Ohio court assessed it (R.C. 3123.17).
- Direct withholding can reach an NC employer without any NC court case (52C-5-501); the contest route is registration + Article 6 contest
  in NC, or a challenge in Ohio.

## Which tribunal hears what
- **Validity of the Ohio order itself (vacate, 60(B), void for lack of service)**: the Ohio issuing court. NC's policy manual notes the
  nonregistering party "can return to the issuing tribunal" to bring whatever contest the issuing state's law allows.
- **Validity/enforceability in NC**: NC district court, within the registration contest, on the 52C-6-607 defenses, including "issuing
  tribunal lacked personal jurisdiction". NC can refuse to confirm or stay NC enforcement; it does not vacate the Ohio order.
- **Ledger/arrears amount**: either: NC contest (payment defense, amount) and/or Ohio court/CSEA (the source ledger). Get the certified Ohio
  payment record first.
- **Modification of current support**: Ohio while Ohio keeps CEJ; NC only under 52C-6-611/613 conditions.
- If the 20-day window passed after valid notice, NC confirmation bars defenses that could have been raised (52C-6-608); whether a defective
  NC notice restarts the window is a question of NC law (unverified; NC lawyer).

## Worked-example checklist: decades-old Ohio order, disputed service, NC collection now (no real names)
Facts pattern: Ohio default support order entered long ago; obligor says they were never served; obligor now lives and works in NC;
NC agency or employer is withholding.

**A. Pull (in this order)**
1. NC paperwork received: notice of registration (date received, how served), IWO copy, any NC hearing notice. Calendar 20 days now.
2. Ohio certified copies: docket/appearance docket, complaint, summons, every return of service (green card signature, sheriff return,
   publication affidavit and the diligence affidavit, certificate of mailing), all journal entries, Civ.R. 58(B) notation.
3. Ohio CSEA: certified payment history from SETS (whole life of the case), list of orders and adjustments, administrative notices.
4. Obligor's proof of residence on the service dates (leases, tax records, employment, school records), kept out of public filings
   unless redacted.

**B. Answer (in this order)**
1. Court vs administrative order? Which court, which county, entry date?
2. Which Civ.R. 4.x method was used, and what did that rule version require on that date? Does the return match it on its face?
3. Did the obligor ever appear, file anything, sign an agreement, or pay in a way that might count as appearance or waiver?
4. Was there personal jurisdiction (Ohio residence then, or a long-arm basis under the then-current 3115/predecessor law)?
5. Any later orders entered after an appearance (these may be valid even if the first is void)?
6. Arrears: does the certified ledger match the orders? Termination date under the order and 3119.86/.861 (or the version in force)?
7. NC: was the registration notice properly served? Is the 20-day window open? Which 52C-6-607 defenses apply (lack of personal
   jurisdiction, payment, limitations, amount)?
8. Remedy map: Ohio motion to vacate as void (+ stay request); NC contest within 20 days asserting 52C-6-607(a)(1) and payment defenses and
   asking for a stay of NC enforcement pending the Ohio ruling; CSEA mistake-of-fact request if a default notice issued.
9. Deadlines and who must be served (obligee, Ohio CSEA, NC agency, employer per 52C-5-506(b)).
10. Risks: waiver by appearing without raising service; confirmation by operation of law; contempt for nonpayment while pending; a
    valid later order; the effect on the child and obligee. Recommend licensed counsel in both states.
