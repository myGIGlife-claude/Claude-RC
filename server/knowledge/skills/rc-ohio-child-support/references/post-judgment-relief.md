Appellate (persuasive only, district-specific), official PDFs read 2026-10-10:
- Margiman v. Dowdell, 2025-Ohio-377 (8th Dist.): restates that where the rules were followed service is presumed, that a sworn statement
  of non-residence at the service address rebuts the presumption and shifts the burden to the plaintiff, and that the common-law motion to
  vacate (not 60(B)) is the vehicle; BUT the movant's affidavit was unsigned, so it had no evidentiary value and the denial of the motion
  to vacate a 2002 default judgment was affirmed. Lesson: a signed, notarized affidavit with specific facts, or nothing.
- One Main Fin. Group, L.L.C. v. Knight, 2026-Ohio-1660 (8th Dist.): commercial-carrier delivery with no signed receipt did not comply
  with Civ.R. 4.1(A)(1)(b), so service could not be presumed; with the defendant's sworn denial, the default judgment was void for lack of
  personal jurisdiction and the denial of her motion to vacate was reversed.
Read the current law of the district where the order was entered before relying on any appellate case; other districts phrase the burden
and the need for a hearing differently.

## Void vs voidable: the test
1. Did the court have subject-matter jurisdiction over this class of case (domestic relations, juvenile parentage/support, CSEA under
   statute)? If yes, mistakes in applying the guideline, deviations, imputation or procedure are voidable: appeal or timely objection only.
2. Did it have personal jurisdiction over the obligor? Obtained by valid service, voluntary appearance, or waiver (failing to raise the
   defense in the first responsive filing). If service was never valid and the obligor never appeared, the judgment is void as to that
   person and can be vacated at any time on the court's inherent power (Patton; Lundeen I ¶ 18).
3. Evidence: the return of service and docket are presumed regular when the rules were followed; the movant needs signed, sworn,
   specific evidence (where they lived, who signed, why the address was wrong), not just "I never got it". A credible affidavit shifts
   the burden, but the trial court still weighs credibility and may deny the motion; whether a hearing must be held varies by district
   (unverified). A defect in service is not automatically a void order: the court decides.
4. A later appearance can waive the defense for future proceedings; analyze each order separately (an order entered after the obligor
   appeared may stand even if the first one falls).
5. Administrative orders: a CSEA order not objected to within 14 days is "final and enforceable" (R.C. 3111.84); attack procedure for a
   void administrative order (unverified): research the court with jurisdiction under 3111.84 and the agency's own rules.

## Civ.R. 60(B) (text unchanged since 1970-07-01)
- Grounds: (1) mistake, inadvertence, surprise, excusable neglect; (2) newly discovered evidence; (3) fraud, misrepresentation or misconduct
  of an adverse party; (4) satisfied/released/discharged, prior judgment reversed, or prospective application no longer equitable;
  (5) any other reason justifying relief.
- Time: "within a reasonable time", and for (1)-(3) not more than one year after entry.
- The motion does not affect finality or suspend operation of the judgment (last paragraph of 60(B)).
- Test: GTE three parts. Not a substitute for appeal (Kuchta).
- Civ.R. 60(A): clerical mistakes corrected any time.
- Paternity-based relief: R.C. 3119.961 applies "notwithstanding" Civ.R. 60(B); mandatory relief if the 3119.962 conditions are met
  (genetic test within six months before the motion showing 0% probability; no adoption; no compliant artificial insemination). Arrears
  cancellation discretionary (3119.964(B)).

## Objections, appeals, stays
- Magistrate's decision: objections within 14 days of filing (Civ.R. 53(D)(3)(b)(i); Juv.R. 40(D)(3)(b)(i)); others 10 days after the first
  objections. Without an objection, factual findings and legal conclusions cannot be assigned as error on appeal except plain error
  (Civ.R. 53(D)(3)(b)(iv)).
- Appeal: notice of appeal within 30 days of a final entry (App.R. 4(A)(1)). If the clerk did not complete Civ.R. 58(B) service of notice of
  the judgment within three days, the 30 days run from actual completion of service (App.R. 4(A)(3)), so check the docket for the 58(B)
  notation on old default orders.
- Tolling (App.R. 4(B)(2)): timely Civ.R. 50(B) or 59 motions, magistrate objections, requests for findings, attorney-fee or prejudgment-
  interest motions. A Civ.R. 60(B) motion is not on the list; denial of 60(B) is separately appealable (unverified as to finality rules in
  each posture; check).
- Stays: trial court may stay execution or enforcement on motion any time after judgment, including while a 60(B) motion is pending
  (Civ.R. 62(A), as amended 2017-07-01); stay on appeal by supersedeas bond (62(B)); ask the trial court first, then the court of appeals
  (App.R. 7(A)). Administrative order objections: order stays in effect unless the court grants a stay (R.C. 3111.84).
- Practical: send a file-stamped copy of any stay to the CSEA and, for income withholding, ask the court/CSEA to issue the amended notice;
  employers follow the withholding notice until told otherwise (R.C. 3121.03 notices).

## Administrative channels
- Default notice: Office of Child Support sends within 15 calendar days after a default is identified (R.C. 3123.03). Mistake-of-fact
  hearing: written request to the CSEA not later than 14 days after the date the default notice is ISSUED (not received); hearing within
  10 days (3123.04). Court hearing: written motion within 14 days after the agency issues its determination; hearing limited to mistake of
  fact (3123.05). Then the determination is final and enforceable (3123.06). (All read on codes.ohio.gov 2026-10-10.)
- Review and adjustment of a court order: CSEA recalculates, mails notice; 14 days to request an administrative hearing; court hearing
  available, and direct to court if a deviation is involved (3119.60-.76; 3119.63(C)-(D)).
- License suspension: notice to obligor first; registrar notice not sooner than 30 days later (3123.54, since 2025-04-09); limited driving
  privileges by motion to the support court (3123.58(B)).
- Federal tools: passport denial when certified arrears exceed $2,500 (42 U.S.C. 652(k)); tax refund offset (R.C. 3123.81-.823 and
  federal offset); license suspension authority (42 U.S.C. 666(a)(16)).

## Arrears: computation, credits, audit
- Each installment, once due, is a judgment by operation of law, entitled to full faith and credit, not retroactively modifiable except back
  to the date notice of a pending modification was given (42 U.S.C. 666(a)(9); R.C. 3119.83-.84).
- Termination of the order does not end the power to collect arrears or to punish contempt (R.C. 3121.36; 3123.14). Withholding to collect
  arrears is presumed at least the amount withheld under the terminated order; deviation factors listed in the statutes.
- While current support runs, arrears payment presumed at least 20% of current support (3123.21).
- Interest: when a court issues or modifies an order and finds a default, it may assess interest at the R.C. 1343.03 rate only if the
  default was willful, from the default date to the new order date (3123.17). No automatic accrual found in the statutes read (other
  interest provisions (unverified)).
- Credits: payments must go through the Office of Child Support (3121.44); direct payments are deemed gifts unless they discharge another
  obligation (3121.45). Claims for direct-payment credit therefore need proof and usually a court finding (outcome varies by district,
  (unverified)).
- Records: SETS is the statewide system (OAC 5101:12-1-15); the CSEA case record lives principally in SETS (5101:12-10-05). Certified
  payment records: ODJFS authorizes CSEA directors/designees to certify SETS payment records as official records (per an ODJFS transmittal
  seen in a search snippet, (unverified) current form). Alternative: Evid.R. 803(6) business record. Under UIFSA a certified payment record
  is admissible to show whether payments were made (R.C. 3115.316(C); N.C. 52C-3-315(c); both verified 2026-10-10).
- Audit steps: (1) list every order with start/end dates and amounts (current, arrears, cash medical, processing charge); (2) compute the
  expected charges month by month; (3) list every payment and its allocation date from the certified record; (4) reconcile to the agency
  balance and flag unexplained adjustments, lump-sum charges, interest, and assigned (public assistance) vs non-assigned arrears;
  (5) check emancipation/termination dates against 3119.86/.861 and the order's own terms.
- Limitation period: Ohio statute of limitations for support arrears (unverified; dormancy statute R.C. 2329.07 read, no support-specific
  rule found). Under UIFSA the longer of the issuing and registering states' periods applies (R.C. 3115.604(B); N.C. 52C-6-604(b)).

## What vacating would and would not undo
| Remedy | Reaches | Does not reach |
|---|---|---|
| Vacate as void (no personal jurisdiction) | The order and arrears accrued under it, as to that party | A later valid order; amounts under a separate order; the underlying duty of support (a new action may follow, and R.C. 3111.13(F)(2)-(3) lets a parentage court order support for the period before its order, with limits); refunds without a further order (unverified, unsettled) |
| Civ.R. 60(B) relief | Whatever the court grants on terms "as are just" | Finality meanwhile; does not stop enforcement by itself |
| Termination of current support | Future installments | Accrued arrears (3121.36) |
| Modification | Installments due after notice of the motion (3119.84) | Earlier arrears (3119.83) |
| Paternity relief (3119.962) | Future support; arrears only if the court cancels (3119.964(B), "may") | Automatic refund: 3119.964(B) only preserves a separate action to recover support paid (procedure and odds unverified) |
| Stay | Enforcement during the stay | The debt itself |
| NC refusal to confirm a registration | NC enforcement of the registered order | The Ohio order; Ohio or other-state enforcement |
