# Historical-order protocol and law timeline (as of 2026-10-10)

Use for any order entered years or decades ago. The law that decides whether the ORIGINAL service and judgment were valid is the law on
those dates. The law that decides what can be done TODAY is current law. Keep two columns in every analysis.

## Protocol (owner's directive section 5, made concrete)
1. Entry date and tribunal of every order (initial, modifications, arrears judgments, administrative orders adopted by a court).
2. Pull: docket/appearance docket, complaint or petition, summons, each return of service, publication or posting affidavits, certificate
   of mailing, magistrate's decision, journal entry, Civ.R. 58(B) notice of judgment, later modifications, CSEA administrative records.
3. For each event, find the statute and rule version in force that day (how-to below).
4. Precedent: what Ohio courts said then and what controls a challenge now (`post-judgment-relief.md`).
5. Separate jurisdiction, service, validity, amount and enforcement.
6. Identify today's procedural route and obstacles (waiver by appearance, res judicata, timeliness, evidence gaps).
7. State what each remedy would reach: the order, future payments, accrued arrears, or only enforcement.
8. Lack of remembered notice is not proof of defective service; a service defect is not automatically a void order. Analyze the return.

## How to get old versions
- **Ohio statutes, 2001 onward (most child support sections)**: codes.ohio.gov section page -> "Available Versions of this Section" ->
  "View <date> Version" opens an authenticated PDF (pattern seen 2026-10-10:
  `codes.ohio.gov/assets/laws/revised-code/authenticated/31/<chapter>/<section>/<M-D-YYYY>/<section>-<M-D-YYYY>.pdf`).
  Many 3119/3121/3123/3125 sections list SB 180 (eff. 2001-03-22) as the earliest version.
- **Before 2001**: the section usually lived elsewhere (Chapter 3113 or 3111). R.C. 3119.01 still cites "division (B) of former section
  3113.21" and "section 3111.21 ... as that section existed prior to January 1, 1998". Get the session law (bill as enrolled) and the LSC
  bill analysis from the General Assembly archive (legislature.ohio.gov; certificate error from this server 2026-10-10, so archive depth
  is (unverified)), or a law library's historical Page's/Baldwin's code. Old-to-new section mapping for SB 180: (unverified).
- **Court rules**: the current Supreme Court of Ohio PDFs list each rule's "Effective Date" and every "Amended" date, with staff notes
  explaining many amendments. For the exact text on a past date, use the staff note plus the Supreme Court's published amendment
  documents or a law library (archive location (unverified)).
- **Agency rules (OAC 5101:12)**: codes.ohio.gov shows current text, effective date and prior effective date; older rule texts are in the
  Register of Ohio filings (registerofohio.state.oh.us) and ODJFS transmittal letters (emanuals.jfs.ohio.gov; refused connection 2026-10-10).
- **North Carolina**: each 52C section ends with its session-law history, e.g. "(1995, c. 538, s. 7(c); 2015-117, s. 1.)". Session laws at
  ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/SessionLaws.

Current mechanics worth checking against any return (2026 text):
- Certified/express mail: return receipt "signed by any person accepting delivery"; clerk dockets mailing and receipt (4.1(A)(2)).
- Personal and residence service: residence service = left at the usual place of residence with a person "of suitable age and discretion
  then residing therein"; failure to serve within 28 days or to file proof does not by itself invalidate service (4.1(B)-(C)).
- Refused (4.6(C)): after clerk notice and a written request, ordinary mail; service complete when mailing is entered of record.
  Unclaimed (4.6(D)): ordinary mail; complete on entry of mailing unless the envelope comes back undelivered.
- Publication (4.4(A)(1)): affidavit of unknown residence and reasonable diligence first; newspaper once a week for six weeks; complete at
  last publication; publisher's affidavit is the proof. Domestic-relations alternative (4.4(A)(2)(a)): posting and mail, available only if
  the requesting party proceeds with a poverty affidavit; six weeks of posting.
- Amendment of process or proof of service is allowed unless it causes material prejudice (4.6(B)).
- Civ.R. 1(C)(5) lists "uniform reciprocal support actions" among proceedings where the Civil Rules may not apply when clearly inapplicable
  (relevant to old URESA cases).

Age-of-termination check for an old order: the 2001 text of 3119.86 already had the high-school continuation and the age-19 cap (then
3119.86(B)); HB 338 moved the cap to 3119.861 in 2025 without changing the high-school ground. For orders before 2001-03-22, read the
predecessor statute in force at entry (unverified location; likely Chapter 3113 / 3109.01 era).
