# Ohio procedure reference  (as of 2026-10)

Read 2026-10-10 from the Supreme Court of Ohio's official PDFs: Civ.R., App.R., Juv.R. (amended 2026-07-01), Evid.R. (2025-07-01),
Sup.R. (amended to 2026-08-06), and the Rules Restructuring Reference Chart. Ohio amends rules every **July 1**; check the staff
notes at the end of each rule for the version in force on your date. Local rules vary by county and division.

## Civil Rules highlights
- **Civ.R. 3**: action commenced by filing **if service is obtained within one year** (3(A)); limited-scope appearance (3(B));
  venue list (3(C)): defendant's residence or business, activity giving rise to the claim, where property is, where the claim arose, etc.
- **Civ.R. 4-4.7 service**: clerk serves by **certified or express mail** with return receipt, or **commercial carrier** with signed
  receipt (4.1(A)); personal service on written request (4.1(B)); residence service; out-of-state (4.3); publication only on
  affidavit of diligence (4.4); foreign (4.5); **refused** certified mail lets the party request ordinary mail service, answer day
  28 days after mailing; **unclaimed** is not refusal (4.6(C)-(D)); waiver (4.7). Service within **6 months** or dismissal without
  prejudice unless good cause (4(E)). Motion to revive a dormant judgment is served like a summons (4(F)).
- **Civ.R. 5**: service on the attorney if represented, both attorney and party for limited appearances; methods: hand, leave at
  office/abode, mail (complete on mailing), commercial carrier (complete on delivery to carrier), clerk if no known address, **email
  or fax to the address given under Civ.R. 11** (complete on transmission, not effective if it failed); **proof of service** stating
  date, manner and the Civ.R. 5(B)(2) subdivision (5(B)(4)); file within 3 days after service (5(D)); e-filing allowed by local
  rule or order (5(E)).
- **Civ.R. 6**: computation (under 7 days skip weekends/holidays); continuances (6(B), absorbs old Sup.R. 41(A): written motion,
  reasons, endorsed by party; no continuance without a new date); responses 14 days, summary judgment 28, replies 7 (6(C));
  +3 days for mail or commercial carrier only (6(D)); no extension of 50(B), 59(B), 59(D), 60(B) times.
- **Civ.R. 7-12**: pleadings allowed (7); motions in writing with grounds and relief (7(B)); notice pleading (8); special matters (9);
  form and caption (10); **Civ.R. 11** signature with address, phone, email; willful violation sanctions; **Civ.R. 12**: answer
  **28 days**; after a Rule 12 motion ruling 14 days; defenses 12(B)(1)-(7); waiver rules (12(G)-(H)).
- **Civ.R. 15**: amend once as of course within 28 days after serving, or 28 days after a responsive pleading or 12(B)/(E)/(F)
  motion; leave freely given; response to amended pleading: later of remaining time or 14 days.
- **Civ.R. 41**: notice dismissal any time **before trial commences** (unless a dependent counterclaim); stipulation; second notice
  dismissal of the same claim is an adjudication on the merits; court-ordered dismissal under (A)(2); involuntary dismissal (B)
  requires notice to plaintiff's counsel (not re-read). The savings statute R.C. 2305.19 may allow refiling (unverified detail).
- **Civ.R. 53 magistrates**: decisions vs orders. **Objections to a magistrate's decision within 14 days of filing**, whether or not
  the court already adopted it; others may object within 10 days after the first objections; objections must be specific; factual
  objections need a transcript or affidavit filed within 30 days; without proper objection only **plain error** can be raised on
  appeal (53(D)(3)(b)). Magistrate's **orders**: motion to set aside, stating reasons with particularity, **within 10 days after
  the order is filed** (53(D)(2)(b)); a motion to set aside does not stay the order. Interim orders on decisions: limited to 28 days
  (53(D)(4)(e)(ii)). The court "shall" allow a reasonable extension for good cause, including the clerk's failure to serve (53(D)(5)).
- **Civ.R. 54(B)**: with multiple claims or parties, a judgment on fewer than all is final only with an express "no just reason for
  delay" determination; otherwise it can be revised any time.
- **Civ.R. 55**: **no clerk default step**: apply to the court; 7 days' written notice of the hearing to a party that appeared; no
  default against minors/incompetents without a representative who appeared; set aside under Civ.R. 60(B); default judgment cannot
  exceed or differ from the demand (54(C)); against the state only on evidence.
- **Civ.R. 56**: claimant may move after the response time; defendant any time; **leave needed once set for pretrial or trial**;
  response per Civ.R. 6(C); evidence limited to listed materials (pleadings, depositions, interrogatory answers, admissions,
  affidavits, transcripts, stipulations); standard: reasonable minds can reach only one conclusion, construed for the nonmovant.
- **Civ.R. 58 (amended 2026-07-01)**: court prepares or asks for the entry; entry **filed and journalized within 30 days** absent
  good cause; judgment effective only when the clerk enters it on the journal; clerk serves notice within 3 days; approval of an
  entry by counsel does not waive objections (58(A)(2)). Clerk failure affects appeal time only as App.R. 4(A) provides.
- **Civ.R. 60**: (A) clerical mistakes (leave of appellate court once appeal docketed); **(B) five grounds**: (1) mistake,
  inadvertence, surprise, excusable neglect; (2) newly discovered evidence; (3) fraud/misconduct of adverse party; (4) satisfied or
  no longer equitable prospectively; (5) any other reason. Reasonable time; **1 year** for (1)-(3). Does not suspend the judgment.
  Test: *GTE Automatic Electric v. ARC Industries*, 47 Ohio St.2d 146 (1976), paragraph two of the syllabus (verified 2026-10-10
  as quoted in *L.N.V. Corp. v. Edgar*, 2012-Ohio-1899, ¶ 18 (12th Dist.)): meritorious claim or defense, a listed ground, timely.
  Failing any prong defeats the motion. Ohio has **no separate "void" ground**: a judgment entered without subject-matter or personal
  jurisdiction is vacated under the court's inherent power, not 60(B), and other errors are voidable (appeal, not 60(B)). The
  authorities (*Patton v. Diemer*; *State v. Harper*; *Ostanek*; *Lundeen v. Turner*) and the void-vs-voidable test live in
  **rc-ohio-child-support** `references/post-judgment-relief.md`: use that file, do not restate the cases from memory.
  A "motion for reconsideration" of a final judgment is a nullity, as is any order on it (*Pitts v. Ohio Dept. of Transp.*, 67
  Ohio St.2d 378 (1981), quoted in *Pine v. Haddox Installation Servs.*, 2005-Ohio-3635, ¶ 9 (9th Dist.), verified 2026-10-10);
  courts may look through the label to 60(B) substance.
- **Civ.R. 62**: no automatic stay. Court may stay on motion pending Civ.R. 50, 59, 60 motions or appeal; on appeal, stay by
  **supersedeas bond** approved by the court (62(B)); government exempt; appellate court powers preserved.
- **Civ.R. 65.1** (civil protection orders) absorbed protection-order notice rules from Sup.R. 10 in 2026.

## Finality and appeals
- **R.C. 2505.02** (current version effective 2025-04-09): final orders include (B)(1) order affecting a substantial right that
  determines the action; (B)(2) substantial right in a special proceeding or summary post-judgment application; (B)(3) vacating a
  judgment or granting a new trial; (B)(4) provisional-remedy orders that determine the remedy and leave no meaningful later
  remedy; (B)(5) class certification; (B)(6)-(7) specific statutes. Add Civ.R. 54(B) language when needed.
- **App.R. 3**: notice filed with the **trial-court clerk** within App.R. 4 time; attach a **time-stamped copy of the judgment**; local
  docketing statement may be required; only the timely notice is jurisdictional, other defects may lead to dismissal. Cross-appeal
  needed to change the order (3(C)). 2026 amendments clarified service of the notice on other parties.
- **App.R. 4**: 30 days (see `deadlines.md`); 4(A)(3) clerk-service delay; 4(B)(1) cross-appeal 10 days; **4(B)(2) tolling list
  excludes Civ.R. 60(B)**; remand procedure when a notice is filed while a tolling motion is pending.
- **App.R. 7**: stay or injunction pending appeal: ask the trial court first (except injunctions); motion in the court of appeals
  shows impracticability or denial, reasons, sworn facts, relevant record parts, notice to parties.
- **App.R. 9-11**: record = original papers, exhibits, transcript, certified docket and journal; appellant must order the needed
  transcript (9(B)); alternatives: statement of evidence (9(C)) or agreed statement (9(D)); record transmitted when complete or
  **40 days** after the notice (20 on accelerated calendar) (10(A)); docketing and accelerated calendar (11, 11.1, 11.2 expedited).
- **App.R. 12**: decide each assignment of error; errors not separately argued may be disregarded.
- **App.R. 14**: computing time; **no extension of App.R. 4 or 25 times**; +3 days for mail/commercial carrier service.
- **App.R. 16**: brief contents in order: table of contents; table of authorities; **assignments of error** with record references;
  issues; statement of the case; facts with record citations; argument per assignment; conclusion with precise relief.
- **App.R. 18**: appellant's brief **20 days** after the clerk's App.R. 11(B) notice; appellee 20 days after; reply 10 days; failure
  to file can mean dismissal (18(C)). App.R. 19 sets format; App.R. 23 frivolous-appeal expenses; App.R. 26 reconsideration and en
  banc (amended 2026: read the current text).
- **Ohio Supreme Court**: jurisdictional appeals and original actions under the S.Ct.Prac.R.: notice of appeal **within 45 days**
  of the court of appeals' judgment entry (S.Ct.Prac.R. 7.01(A)(1), read 2026-10-10 on the 2025-05-01 rules); a timely App.R. 26
  reconsideration or en banc application restarts the 45 days from the ruling on it (7.01(A)(5)-(6)); the memorandum in support of
  jurisdiction is due with the notice or the appeal is dismissed.

## Juvenile Rules basics (Juv.R., amended 2026-07-01)
- Separate rule set for delinquency, unruly, abuse/neglect/dependency, custody in juvenile court: complaint (10), service (15-16),
  time (18), motions (19), service of papers (20), discovery (24), adjudicatory hearing (29), dispositional hearing (34), magistrates
  (**Juv.R. 40**, same 14-day objection rule as Civ.R. 53). New **Juv.R. 9(C)** pre-initial-appearance diversion (2026-07-01).
- Juvenile records and names are protected; use initials (Juv.R. 5) and check confidentiality statutes before filing exhibits.

## Evidence Rules (Evid.R., amended 2025-07-01: Evid.R. 615 victim presence)
- 101 scope (remote presence by live two-way video defined); 201 judicial notice; 401-403; 801-807 hearsay; **803(6)** business
  records shown by custodian testimony **or as provided by 901(B)(10)**; **901** authentication; **902(11)-(14)** certified domestic and
  foreign business records, records generated by an electronic process, and data copied from an electronic device (added 2020);
  1002-1004 original-writing rule. Evid.R. 101(D) lists proceedings where the rules do not apply (check before arguing hearsay).

- **Sup.R. 11.13**: the filer must omit personal identifiers and file them on a separate form; clerk does not screen and cannot reject
  for that reason. Personal identifiers (11.09(H)): SSN except last 4; financial account numbers incl. card numbers; employer and
  employee ID numbers; a juvenile's name in abuse/neglect/dependency cases (use initials or a generic abbreviation).
- **Sup.R. 11.14**: written motion to restrict public access to information in a case document (balancing test in the rule).
- Local AI rules exist: e.g., Montgomery County Common Pleas Loc.R. 12.08 (dated 2025-11-19, read 2026-10-10) requires attorneys and
  self-represented litigants to disclose any generative-AI use at filing, in an attached certification that the filer reviewed the
  material for accuracy, validity and relevancy; the court may strike or disregard the filing and impose Civ.R. 11-type sanctions
  (including dismissal) and refer attorneys for discipline. Hamilton County Loc.R. 49 is similar (not opened).

## Finding local rules and e-filing
- Each court posts its local rules; courts must file them with the Clerk of the Supreme Court (Sup.R. 3.00). Check the division
  (general, domestic relations, juvenile, probate) and the magistrate's own standing orders. E-filing is county by county.
- Local discovery rules often set meet-and-confer certificates, limits on interrogatories, and motion-to-compel prerequisites.

## Sanctions, vexatious litigators, entities
- **R.C. 2323.51** frivolous conduct (fees and costs on motion; details not re-read); **Civ.R. 11** willful violations;
  **App.R. 23** frivolous appeal.
- **R.C. 2323.52** (eff. 2025-04-09): declaration as a vexatious litigator in common pleas (filed while the conduct's cases are
  pending or within one year after); then **leave required** to institute or continue proceedings or file applications in the court of
  claims, common pleas, municipal and county courts (court-of-appeals filings need separate leave); filings without leave are
  dismissed; the Supreme Court publishes the list; public-records requests also need leave.
- **R.C. 1925.17**: in small claims, a corporation may file and present through a bona fide officer or salaried employee, but no
  cross-examination, argument or advocacy without a lawyer. Elsewhere entities need counsel (R.C. 4705.01 practice-of-law rule; not re-read).

## Extraordinary writs (outline only; stringent)
- Mandamus (R.C. Chapter 2731), prohibition, procedendo, habeas corpus (R.C. Chapter 2725), quo warranto: original actions in the
  court of appeals or the Supreme Court. Generally require a clear legal right, a clear legal duty, and **no adequate remedy in the
  ordinary course of law** (appeal usually is one). Inmates must file the R.C. 2969.25 affidavits (not re-read). Read the S.Ct.Prac.R.
  original-action rules and recent writ decisions before drafting; most writ petitions fail on the adequate-remedy element.
