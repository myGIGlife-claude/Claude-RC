# North Carolina procedure reference  (as of 2026-10)

N.C. R. Civ. P. (G.S. 1A-1), Rules of Evidence (G.S. 8C-1) and statutes read on ncleg.gov 2026-10-10. N.C. R. App. P. read 2026-10-10
from the **2025-09-02 codification** on nccourts.gov (the site returns 403 to plain fetches; a browser user-agent works). The rules
page listed only the 2025-03-19 (Rules 14, 15, 16, App. A, D) and 2025-08-20 (Rule 36) orders; open it again before relying on a rule.

## Civil Procedure (G.S. 1A-1)
- **Rule 3**: action commenced by filing a complaint, or by summons plus a court order allowing the complaint within 20 days.
  Attorneys e-file where available under General Rules of Practice Rule 5; registered self-represented litigants may e-file.
- **Rule 4**: summons issued within 5 days of filing; served in NC by the sheriff or other authorized person, outside NC by a
  non-party 21+ (4(a)); **personal or substituted service within 60 days of issuance** (4(c)); keep the action alive by
  **endorsement or alias/pluries summons within 90 days** (4(d)); manner of service (4(j)): natural persons by delivery, abode,
  agent, **registered/certified mail return receipt**, designated delivery service, and further options (4(j)(1)a-d read; rest not);
  entities by officer, director, managing agent or registered agent and mail options (4(j)(6), not re-read); publication only
  after diligence (4(j1)).
- **Rule 5**: e-service through the court's system on attorneys at their email of record; on parties only if they consented and the
  consent is filed; **sent after 5:00 p.m. ET = next business day**; otherwise delivery, fax, email of record, mail (5(b));
  certificate of service required (5(b)(2)-(3), not re-read); in superior court, briefs for or against a motion to dismiss, for
  judgment on the pleadings, for summary judgment or any other dispositive motion must be served **at least 2 days before the
  hearing**, or the court may continue it (5(a1)).
- **Rule 6**: computation (under 7 days skip weekends/holidays); enlargement before expiry for cause, after expiry for excusable
  neglect; **parties may stipulate up to 30 days total** without the court (6(b)); no extension of Rules 50(b), 52, 59(b),(d),(e),
  60(b); motion and notice served **5 days** before hearing, opposing affidavits **2 days** before (6(d)); **+3 days for mail** (6(e)).
- **Rule 7**: pleadings; motions in writing with particular grounds and relief (7(b)(1), not re-read).
- **Rule 8(a)(2)**: in negligence actions and punitive-damage claims over $25,000 the pleading must **not** state the dollar demand,
  only that damages exceed $25,000 (any party may then demand a written statement of the amount within 30 days, not filed until
  trial); in G.S. 7A-45.4(a) subject-matter cases state whether $5,000,000 or more is claimed. Check before drafting a prayer.
- **Rule 11**: attorney or unrepresented party signs and states an address; pleadings need not be verified unless a rule or
  statute says so; signature certifies reasonable inquiry, factual and legal grounds, proper purpose; violation means the court
  **shall** impose an appropriate sanction (on signer, represented party, or both). Verification rules in 11(b)-(d).
- **Rule 12**: answer **30 days** after service; after a motion ruling **20 days**; defenses 12(b)(1)-(7) incl. "improper venue or
  division"; after federal remand 30 days from filing the certified remand order (12(a)(2)).
- **Rule 15**: amend once as of course before a responsive pleading (or within 30 days if none is permitted and not on the trial
  calendar); otherwise leave or written consent; leave freely given.
- **Rule 41**: voluntary dismissal by notice **any time before the plaintiff rests**, or stipulation; without prejudice unless stated;
  second notice dismissal of the same claim is an adjudication; **refile within one year** after a voluntary dismissal without
  prejudice of a timely action (unless a stipulation shortens it).
- **Rule 50(b)**: JNOV motion **10 days** after entry of judgment. **Rule 52**: findings required in bench trials; on motions only
  if requested; amend findings within **10 days** (52(b)).
- **Rule 55**: clerk enters default on affidavit or motion (55(a)); clerk judgment for sum certain (verified pleading may replace the
  affidavit); otherwise judge, with **3 days'** notice to a party who appeared (55(b)(2)); set aside entry for good cause, judgment under
  Rule 60(b) (55(d)).
- **Rule 56**: claimant may move after **30 days from commencement**; defendant any time; motion served **10 days** before the
  hearing; opposing affidavits **2 days** before; standard: no genuine issue of material fact and entitled to judgment as a matter of law.
- **Rule 58**: judgment entered when **reduced to writing, signed by the judge, and filed** with the clerk; the designated party serves
  it within **3 days**; noncompliance tolls Rule 50(b), 52(b), 59 times up to 90 days.
- **Rule 59**: nine grounds (59(a)); motion served **10 days** after entry (59(b)). Commentary warns Rule 59 is for post-trial
  judgments; misuse may not toll the appeal clock.
- **Rule 60**: (a) clerical; (b) six grounds incl. **(4) the judgment is void**; reasonable time, **one year** for (1)-(3); no
  suspension of the judgment; independent action and fraud on the court preserved.
- **Rule 62**: **no execution until the time to give notice of appeal expires** (62(a)); injunction and receivership judgments not
  stayed unless ordered; discretionary stays pending Rules 50, 52(b), 59, 60 motions (62(b)); injunction pending appeal (62(c)).

## Appellate Procedure (2025-09-02 codification)
- **Rule 3** (civil): notice filed with the **clerk of superior court** and served on all parties; **30 days** after entry if served
  within Rule 58's 3 days, else 30 days after service; tolled only by timely **Rules 50(b), 52(b), 59** motions, then 30 days from
  entry (or late service) of the disposing order; **Rule 6(e) and App. R. 27(b) added days never apply**; cross-appeal 10 days after
  service of the first notice; content: appealing parties, the judgment or order, the court appealed to, signature of counsel or
  the unrepresented party (3(d)). Timeliness is jurisdictional (*Booth v. Utica Mut. Ins. Co.*, 308 N.C. 187, 189 (1983)); App. R.
  27(c) forbids extending it; the only fallback is a discretionary certiorari petition (21(a)(1)).
- **Rule 3.1**: juvenile-code appeals (G.S. 7B-1001), expedited. **Rule 4** (criminal): oral notice at trial or written notice within
  **14 days** after entry or after a ruling on a timely motion for appropriate relief.
- **Rule 7**: transcripts ordered by **Appellate Division Transcript Contract**; appellant serves it **within 14 days** after the notice
  of appeal (appellee within 28 days).
- **Rule 8**: stay pending appeal: first by security with the clerk where law allows or by trial-court motion; then temporary stay and
  writ of supersedeas in the appellate court (Rule 23).
- **Rule 9**: review is solely on the record on appeal (printed record, transcripts, exhibits, supplements).
- **Rule 10**: **preservation**: timely request, objection or motion with specific grounds and a ruling; jury-charge objections before
  the jury retires; some issues (subject-matter jurisdiction, judgment supported by findings) preserved by law.
- **Rule 11**: settle the record: by agreement within 45 days after transcripts are delivered or the last notice of appeal (whichever
  later); otherwise appellant serves a proposed record; appellee objects or proposes alternatives within 30 days; judicial settlement.
- **Rule 13**: appellant's brief **30 days** after the record is filed (60 in death cases); appellee 30 days after service of appellant's
  brief (60 in death cases); reply under Rule 28(h); a missing appellant's brief can mean dismissal (13(c)). **Rule 27**: time computed
  like Rule 6 (last day on a weekend or courthouse-closed holiday rolls forward); +3 days for mail or permitted e-mail service except
  for the notice of appeal (27(b)); trial court may extend once, up to 30 days, only the Rule 7 transcript-delivery and Rule 11/18
  proposed-record times (27(c)(1)); everything else goes to the appellate court; no extension of the time to appeal, PDR or rehearing.
- **Rule 14**: appeal of right to the Supreme Court (G.S. 7A-30): notice within **15 days after the Court of Appeals mandate** issues
  (amended 2025-04-01). Rule 15: discretionary review petitions (amended 2025-04-01).
- **Rule 28**: brief function and content; issues not presented in the brief, or presented without reason or argument, "will be
  taken as abandoned"; each issue states its standard of review (28(b)(6)).
- **Rule 33**: attorneys; **Rule 33.1**: secure leave periods for attorneys. There is **no Rule 33A** in the current numbering.
- **Rule 34**: sanctions for frivolous appeals (not well grounded, improper purpose, grossly deficient filings).
- **Rule 37**: motions in appellate courts (filed with the clerk, served on all parties, any time before the case is called unless a
  rule sets another time); response **within 10 days after service** or before the case is called for argument, whichever is
  shorter (37(a)); the court may act before a response (37(b)); motions should report a good-faith effort to learn whether the
  other parties consent (37(c)). Rules 38 substitution of parties, 39 clerks' duties and office hours.

## Evidence (G.S. 8C-1)
- Mirrors the FRE numbering with NC differences. **803(6)** business records: custodian or qualified witness, **or affidavit, or a
  certification complying with 28 U.S.C. 1746**, but affidavit authentication is **limited to nonparty records** and needs advance
  notice. Rule 902 self-authentication (domestic public documents, certified copies). Rule 201 judicial notice. Check 8C-1 for
  differences in 404(b), expert testimony (702) and hearsay exceptions before reusing federal arguments.

## Family-court pointers (district court)
- **Chapter 50**: divorce, alimony, child custody and support (G.S. 50-13.x). **Chapter 50A**: Uniform Child-Custody Jurisdiction and
  Enforcement Act (custody jurisdiction and registration). **Chapter 52C**: Uniform Interstate Family Support Act (registering,
  enforcing, modifying out-of-state support orders; controlling-order rules). **Chapter 50B**: domestic-violence protective orders.
- Local family-court rules by judicial district (custody mediation, financial affidavits, scheduling) control much of the practice:
  read the district's local rules first.

## E-filing and local rules
- **eCourts statewide since 2025-10-13**: Enterprise Justice (Odyssey) case management; **File & Serve** for e-filing and e-service;
  **Guide & File** for self-represented interviews; Portal for search and payment. General Rules of Practice Rule 5 governs e-filing.
- Local rules and administrative orders: nccourts.gov local rules by county/district. AI: Superior Court District 25 (Cabarrus
  County) Revised Administrative Order 25-09 (2025-12-08, replacing a 2024 order): per the UNC School of Government it requires
  disclosure of AI used to generate or alter **evidence** (generally 90 days before trial), not of AI used to draft advocacy
  documents (order text not opened). No statewide AI filing rule found (unverified absence). **W.D.N.C.**: standing order of
  2024-06-18 (per NC Lawyers Weekly and a law-firm tracker; order not opened) requires a certification on filings, including pro
  se ones, that no AI beyond the research platforms' built-in tools was used and that a person checked every statement and cite.

## Unsworn declarations, privacy, sanctions, entities
- **G.S. 7A-98**: declaration under penalty of perjury replaces a sworn statement **only for matters e-filed under Supreme Court
  rules**; excluded: oral testimony, oaths of office, statements required before an official other than a notary, wills under
  G.S. 31-11.6, instruments needing acknowledgment under G.S. 47-17. False declarations are perjury (G.S. 14-209, Class F).
- **G.S. 132-1.10(d)**: no SSN, employer TIN, driver's license, state ID, passport, bank account, credit/debit card numbers, or PINs
  and passwords in court filings unless required by law or order, or redacted; any person can ask the clerk to redact a web image (f).
- **G.S. 6-21.5**: fees when the losing party's pleading had a complete absence of a justiciable issue; written findings required.
- **G.S. 84-5**: corporations may not practice law (attorneys they retain may represent them).
- **G.S. 1-277**: interlocutory appeal only for orders affecting a substantial right etc.; adverse personal-jurisdiction rulings
  immediately appealable (1-277(b)). G.S. 1-279.1: appeal by notice under the appellate rules.
