# Deadline computation: federal vs Ohio vs North Carolina  (as of 2026-10)

Read on the official texts 2026-10-10: FRCP/FRAP (uscourts.gov, via law.cornell.edu), Ohio Civ.R./App.R. effective 2026-07-01,
N.C. G.S. 1A-1 (ncleg.gov), N.C. R. App. P. 2025-09-02 codification (nccourts.gov). Local rules and orders can change response times.
Label every computed date **verified / provisional / unknown**, show the arithmetic, and name the rule.

## 1. Counting rules
| Question | Federal (FRCP 6, FRAP 26) | Ohio (Civ.R. 6, App.R. 14) | North Carolina (Rule 6) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day of the trigger | excluded | excluded | excluded |
| Weekends/holidays inside the period | always counted | counted, **except** periods under 7 days: skip intermediate Sat/Sun/holidays | trial court same as Ohio: under 7 days skip them (Rule 6(a)); **App. R. 27(a) has no such exception**: count every day on appeal |
| Last day on weekend/holiday | roll to next day that is not | roll forward | roll forward ("legal holiday when the courthouse is closed for transactions") |
| Clerk's office inaccessible | 6(a)(3): next accessible day | Civ.R. 6(A): office closed all day or before usual closing: next day | (unverified) check G.S. 1A-1 Rule 6 and AOC closure orders |
| When the last day ends | e-filing: midnight court time; paper: clerk closing (6(a)(4)); local rule can differ | court's e-filing local rule; paper: clerk hours | e-service through the system after 5:00 p.m. ET counts as next business day (Rule 5(b)); e-filing cutoff per General Rules of Practice Rule 5 (unverified) |
| Hours-based periods | 6(a)(2): count every hour | not addressed | not addressed |
| Legal holidays | 6(a)(6): federal list incl. Juneteenth, plus state holidays for forward-counted periods | R.C. 1.14 list (unverified detail) | state holidays when courthouse closed |

## 2. Extra days after service
| | Federal FRCP 6(d) | Ohio Civ.R. 6(D) / App.R. 14(C) | NC Rule 6(e) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mail | +3 | +3 | +3 |
| Commercial carrier | only if consented under 5(b)(2)(F) | +3 | no |
| Leaving with clerk | +3 | no | no |
| Electronic (court system or email) | **no** (since 2016) | no | trial court: no (Rule 6(e) is mail only); appellate App. R. 27(b): +3 for mail **or e-mail where the rules allow it** |
| Exceptions | applies only to periods after **service** | not for answering a summons; App.R. 14(C) not when the court set a date | **never** for the notice of appeal: App. R. 3(c) switches off both Rule 6(e) and App. R. 27(b) |

Trap: the extra days attach only when the clock runs from **service**. Appeal clocks run from **entry** (federal, Ohio), so no add-on.

## 6. Tolling: what restarts the appeal clock
- **Federal FRAP 4(a)(4)(A)**: timely 50(b), 52(b), 54 fees (only if the court orders under FRCP 58(e)), 59, and 60 **if filed within the
  59 period (28 days)**. A later notice is needed to challenge the ruling on the motion (4(a)(4)(B)(ii)). The time limit is
  jurisdictional (*Bowles v. Russell*, 551 U.S. 205 (2007), verified 2026-10-10); FRCP 6(b)(2) forbids extending 50(b), 52(b), 59, 60(b) times.
- **Ohio App.R. 4(B)(2)**: timely Civ.R. 50(B), 59, objections to a magistrate's decision, requests for findings (Civ.R. 52 or
  53(D)(3)(a)(ii)), motion for attorney fees, motion for prejudgment interest. **Civ.R. 60(B) is not on the list.** A notice filed
  while one is pending triggers remand and stay; then amend the notice or file a new one.
- **NC App. R. 3(c)(3)**: timely Rules 50(b), 52(b), 59 only; the 30 days then run from entry of the order disposing of the motion
  (or its late service). **Rule 60 is not on the list.** An improper Rule 59 motion (for example a disguised reconsideration motion
  or one attacking a non-trial order) may not toll at all (UNC SOG commentary; check cases). A late notice is a jurisdictional defect:
  *Booth v. Utica Mut. Ins. Co.*, 308 N.C. 187, 189 (1983).
- Universal trap: an untimely or improper post-judgment motion does not toll; a motion for reconsideration is not a tolling motion
  unless it fits a listed rule. If in doubt, file the notice of appeal on time and pursue the motion in parallel where the rules allow.

## 7. Worked example (pattern only; dates are illustrative)
Ohio judgment journalized on a Tuesday; clerk serves notice the next day (inside 3 days). Day 0 = journalization day. Count 30
calendar days; if day 30 is Saturday, file Monday (if not a holiday). A Civ.R. 60(B) motion filed on day 20 changes nothing: the
notice of appeal is still due on day 30. A timely Civ.R. 59 motion filed on day 20 restarts the 30 days from the entry resolving it.
